### Jared Kushner Hacked Phone Reveals Secret Negotiations, Millions in Transactions

Journalist Matthew Cole alleges in his forthcoming book, “Princes and Thieves,” that Jared Kushner gained access to at least $2 billion provided by Emirati and Saudi leaders during President Donald Trump’s first administration. Cole describes alleged financial arrangements involving UAE ruler Mohamed bin Zayed and Saudi leader Mohammed bin Salman that combined off-the-books diplomacy with funds reportedly intended for Kushner’s personal benefit. Cole reports that Dutch military intelligence gained visibility into Kushner’s iPhone screen in early 2018 after discovering screenshots on a Russian intelligence server. Dutch surveillance allegedly revealed a UAE account receiving hundreds of millions of dollars through an Azerbaijani bank. Cole says roughly $750 million flowed into an account owned by the Emirati charity Watani Al Emarat Foundation, with Kushner serving as a signatory, to finance covert foreign-policy objectives. Cole separately alleges that roughly $1 billion reached an Israeli central bank account intended for Kushner’s personal benefit. Cole reports that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu required advance notice of transactions and that some funds subsequently moved through Cyprus. Cole also describes a separate UAE-funded account intended to cover Israeli assistance against a major Iranian attack. The alleged arrangements raise potential financial-disclosure, foreign-agent and bribery issues, according to Cole’s reporting and a former Mueller investigation prosecutor quoted in the book. Kushner’s counsel denied that the accounts existed, denied any connection to the Emirati charity or UAE account, and said Kushner complied with the law. Former US ambassador to Israel David M. Friedman categorically denied allegations that he relayed messages concerning the alleged Israeli account. ### 1Password launches a way for AI agents to log in on your behalf without seeing your password

1Password launched 1Password for Agents on Thursday, allowing users to authorize AI agents to log into personal accounts without exposing credentials to the agents. The service verifies an agent’s digital identity, asks the user to approve the login and fills in credentials on the agent’s behalf. 1Password says the platform supports agents from Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Meta and AI assistant startup Town, with additional integrations planned. The company positions the service as an alternative to sharing passwords that agents could retain beyond a user’s control. The launch coincides with an Axios report that U.S. banks are detecting AI agents operating inside customer accounts. The report identifies a central concern: banks often cannot determine whether users authorized the activity. A Chase spokesperson warned customers against sharing usernames and passwords. ([1Password launches a way for AI agents to log in on your behalf without seeing your password](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/1password-launches-a-way-for-ai-agents-to-log-in-on-your-behalf-without-seeing-your-password/)).

### The next-gen Xbox won’t cost you $1,800. It can’t. Can it?

Microsoft’s next Xbox, Project Helix, could cost consumers between $1,200 and $1,800 when it arrives in 2028, according to Microsoft insiders cited by Fortune. The reported projection is not an announced price, and Fortune says Microsoft could lower the cost through subsidies. Xbox leaders have publicly warned that consoles costing thousands of dollars cannot reach mass audiences. However, Xbox boss Asha Sharma has also called Microsoft’s spending on content, platform and hardware subsidies unsustainable. Helix’s planned support for PC games could complicate hardware subsidies because purchases through competing storefronts such as Steam would not help Microsoft recover its investment. Sean Hollister argues that Microsoft could reconcile an expensive flagship with its affordability promises through subscription bundles, cloud gaming, continued support for existing consoles and cheaper partner-made devices. A long-term Game Pass commitment could spread hardware costs over time, although Microsoft has not announced such an arrangement. Xbox’s newly launched pay-as-you-go cloud gaming offers another route to access without buying a console. A costly Helix could also remain an early-adopter product rather than become an essential upgrade. Hollister argues that developers would have little incentive to prioritize hardware that most players cannot afford, potentially extending the current generation. Former gaming executives Mike Ybarra, Shuhei Yoshida and Shawn Layden have questioned the need for another major hardware leap. Sharma has described Helix as a “family of devices,” raising the possibility of less powerful, cheaper Xbox hardware from PC partners. Microsoft has also discussed flexible storage options. Hollister’s central argument is that a high flagship price would not necessarily mean every Xbox player must pay it, but Microsoft’s alternatives could involve subscription commitments, cloud access or remaining on older hardware. ([The next-gen Xbox won’t cost you $1,800. It can’t. Can it?](https://www.theverge.com/games/1016134/xbox-helix-price-1800-isnt-affordable-editorial)).

### Turning Database Grading into RL Rewards: Post-Training Agents with ThinkingBox

Microsoft and Hugging Face have announced ThinkingBox-Training, a released training stack that rewards AI agents for leaving backend databases in the correct state rather than merely claiming success. After 50 steps of reinforcement learning, Qwen3.8-27B’s average success on ThinkingBox-Bench rose from 51.70% to 60.78%, while the number of tasks completed correctly on all 20 recorded attempts increased from 38 to 107. The improvement came with a coverage tradeoff: the number of benchmark tasks solved at least once fell from 453 to 446. The results distinguish repeatability from occasional success, although passing 20 recorded attempts does not establish guaranteed reliability. The researchers trained the model on 187 tasks excluded from the 507-task benchmark, using repeated base-model trials to select tasks that produced a mixture of successes and failures. Most rewards came from deterministic checks of final database state, while a minority used an LLM judge for narrow requirements. The reference training run used 24 NVIDIA H100 GPUs. The benchmark tested unfamiliar domains, databases and most tools, but training and evaluation shared the ThinkingBox framework, simulated-user machinery and task style. Those similarities limit the breadth of the transfer claim. The researchers estimated that evaluation cost per task passing all 20 attempts fell from $24.36 to $8.00. Those figures are comparative inference-cost estimates, not actual bills, and exclude training and task-selection costs. The public repository includes training infrastructure and sample tasks, but not the private 187-task pool used for the reported results. ([Turning Database Grading into RL Rewards: Post-Training Agents with ThinkingBox](https://huggingface.co/blog/microsoft/thinkingbox-training)).

## Tools and Smaller Signals

- [Build more responsive apps with the Decisions API](https://openai.com/index/decisions-api): OpenAI has made its Decisions API generally available, using GPT‑6 Luna to handle bounded decisions such as classification, routing and next-action selection. OpenAI says the API processes text, images and structured inputs up to 10 times faster than GPT‑6 Luna through the Responses API. Developers define a question and its allowed answers, and the API returns a probability, a predefined choice or a rubric-based score. The service now supports native JSON inputs, images in question instructions and up to 400 questions per request, double the previous limit. New features include prompt caching, US and EU inference residency, and native GPT‑Live integration for decisions during voice conversations. Applications can use the integration to route support requests, trigger escalation or adjust an avatar’s expression as someone speaks. Pricing starts at $0.10 per million input tokens, with cached input priced at $0.01 per million tokens and no output-token charges. Zero Data Retention is available to eligible customers.

- [This design software reimagines everyday objects as self-aware devices](https://news.mit.edu/2026/design-software-reimagines-everyday-objects-as-self-aware-devices-1022): Researchers at MIT’s Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and Tianjin University have developed X-Hinges, software for designing 3D-printed objects with integrated motion sensors. X-Hinges uses separate conductive materials for sensors and wire-like connections to reduce signal interference and support more precise sensing. Users can customize shapes and select movement along up to three axes, while the software automatically embeds sensors at bending points. Demonstrations include a glove that relays finger movements to a robotic hand, a folding lamp that adjusts brightness when pinched, a tactile object-recognition device, and a shark-shaped game controller. Lead author Xiang Chang and senior author Jiaji Li developed a computer vision system to recalibrate sensor outputs because small fabrication differences can cause apparently identical objects to produce different readings. The researchers hope to build signal recalibration into future devices and combine sensing with actuation in a single print. The researchers will present X-Hinges at the ACM Symposium on User Interface Software and Technology in early November.
- [Quad9 (DNS Resolver) to Exit France](https://quad9.net/news/blog/au-revoir-france/): Quad9 will withdraw its free DNS and cybersecurity services from France on November 24, 2026, following a French court ruling requiring it to block domain names for users in the country. The Swiss nonprofit said the October 21 ruling favored beIN in a dispute over domains allegedly infringing sports broadcasting rights. The court reduced the daily penalty from €580,000 to €1,000, according to Quad9. The foundation said approximately 20 additional lawsuits in France create financial risks it cannot sustain. Quad9 argues that geographically targeted blocking cannot reliably establish users’ jurisdictions and conflicts with its privacy commitments. The foundation rejects worldwide blocking because that approach would apply a French court’s restrictions to users elsewhere. Quad9 also argues that DNS blocking does not remove infringing content and can be circumvented by switching resolvers. Quad9 said its French users include government, education, healthcare, utility and private-sector networks, although its network-level statistics do not identify individual users or reliably establish jurisdiction. An appeal remains pending, and the foundation plans to continue challenging court-ordered DNS blocking.
- [OpenShell – A safe, private runtime for fleets of autonomous AI agents](https://github.com/NVIDIA/OpenShell): NVIDIA’s OpenShell 0.1.x introduces a stable release cadence, new isolation primitives, expanded extensibility and new APIs for a runtime designed to constrain autonomous AI agents. OpenShell enforces declared policies through kernel-level controls on file access, system calls and network connections. OpenShell keeps real credentials out of agents’ reach and adds credentials only to requests bound for approved endpoints. OpenShell also uses formal verification to identify risky access introduced by proposed policy changes and hold those changes for human review. OpenShell supports Linux, Apple Silicon macOS and experimental Windows support through WSL 2, with Docker, Podman or host virtualization required. OpenShell provides Python, TypeScript, Go and Rust SDKs, alongside Kubernetes gateway deployment and extension interfaces for middleware, interceptors and compute drivers. OpenShell is licensed under Apache 2.0 and collects optional anonymous operational telemetry. OpenShell says its telemetry excludes prompts, credentials, file paths, model names and user content.
