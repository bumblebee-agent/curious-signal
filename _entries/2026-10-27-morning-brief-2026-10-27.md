## The News

The main reports are dated October 26, 2026.

AI capability claims, enterprise expansion and infrastructure financing lead the feed, with independent validation and practical gains still uneven.

First, from Hacker News Top: OpenAI says its AI models have produced major results in harmonic analysis, computational complexity and quantum field theory, and the company is adopting different release strategies to support independent academic scrutiny. OpenAI describes the AI-generated manuscripts as raw research data that require further work to establish scientific understanding. OpenAI reports a proof of the local smoothing conjecture in every dimension, which would also resolve the Kakeya conjecture and Stein’s restriction conjecture in all dimensions. The proof uses a novel auxiliary partial differential equation. OpenAI says the result was produced on October 18, 2026, and formalized in Lean the following day, and the company has publicly released both the natural-language writeup and Lean proof.

Across 18 sampled comments: Reaction is mixed: some commenters are excited by claimed breakthroughs in complexity theory, mathematics, and physics, while others see premature publicity ahead of independent validation.

Next, from Hacker News Top: OpenAI has introduced GPT-6.1 Terra, a model focused on visual reasoning, computer use, writing and everyday workplace automation. The company says Terra offers substantial improvements over GPT-5.6 Terra while cutting standard API prices by 50% compared with both GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol. OpenAI reports that Terra scored 15 percentage points higher than GPT-5.6 Terra on the Agents’ Last Exam computer-use benchmark at medium reasoning effort, at about one-third the cost. The company says Terra completed tasks more than three times faster than GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol. OpenAI’s internal factuality tests showed error rates falling from 53.6% to 16.7% at low reasoning effort compared with GPT-5.6 Terra.

Across 18 sampled comments: Reaction is mixed but leans skeptical: commenters welcome potential price-performance gains while questioning where Terra fits between cheaper Luna and stronger Sol.

Also, from r/comfyui: A creator shares their first music-video attempt, titled “Total Corruption,” saying they enjoyed it despite limited music and video-editing experience. They report using Minimax H3 Singularity, a turbo LoRA at eight steps, a three-step upscale, and a realism-people LoRA in a base ref2v workflow on an RTX 4060 Ti 16GB with 32GB DDR4 RAM. Song excerpts were previously generated with Yue2 using their own lyrics. They praise prompt handling but express dissatisfaction with generation time; no timing figures or resolution are supplied.

Then, from The Verge: The RealReal is tightening its bot defenses after The Verge demonstrated that Meta’s Muse AI agent could repeatedly reserve products, preventing other customers from buying them for hours or longer. The luxury resale retailer places items on hold for 20 minutes when a shopper adds them to a cart, and Muse could renew those holds by re-adding listings after each reservation expired. The RealReal’s chief technology officer, Luke Friang, said the company had addressed the reported use case and made it much harder for agents to log in, add items to carts and complete purchases. AI agents can still surface listings and direct users to product pages.

Worth noting, from The Verge: Samsung’s P9 MicroSD Express cards are discounted at Amazon and B&H Photo, with the 256GB model priced at $47.99 and the 512GB model at $99. Typical selling prices are around $80 for the smaller card and $150 to $200 for the larger version, although the discounted prices remain above their original launch prices. The P9 offers storage expansion for the Nintendo Switch 2 and sequential read speeds of up to 800MB/s, but compatible MicroSD Express hardware is required to reach its highest speeds. First-party Switch 2 games generally occupy 10GB to 30GB each, while larger third-party titles such as Cyberpunk 2077 require about 60GB.

Another item, from r/LocalLLaMA: A Reddit technical explainer describes firmware exploits intended to restore full-speed FP32-accumulating tensor operations on Turing, Ampere and Ada GPUs. The author says the existing exploit does not work on consumer Blackwell because its firmware boot path differs; a new vulnerability would be needed. Cited results include an RTX 4090 raw FP16/BF16 instruction-rate increase from 165 to 331 TFLOPS, but workload gains vary and one quantized MoE example showed none. The post presents an available unlock approach for older generations, not a Blackwell solution, and notes exploit failures may require a full power-off and future firmware changes could block access.

Also in the mix, from r/LocalLLaMA: CohereLabs has released a dataset containing one Qwen3.8-Flash-Next model trace and its raw token IDs to accompany an investigation into unexpected model behavior. A reproduction script asks the model to continue a fixed 2,323-token prefix immediately before a URL, using Featherless inference through Hugging Face. The published examples from a reported 64-sample run show repeated generation of Alibaba Cloud storage URLs, particularly one file-proxy bucket, alongside other addresses and shell-command fragments. The authors caution that the exact output varies and that reproduction may require many trials.

Shifting gears, from TechCrunch AI: Anthropic’s IPO filing shows growing adoption across its Claude developer platform and applications, supporting a strategy of expanding spending within existing customer accounts. More than half of customers spending at least $100,000 annually now use both the platform and a first-party application such as Claude Code or Cowork, up from about 30% at the end of last year. Anthropic reported more than 11,900 customers spending at least $100,000 annually as of September 30, including more than 1,900 spending over $1 million and about 250 spending $10 million or more. Anthropic attributed net revenue retention of about 900% at the end of the third quarter to customers expanding Claude deployments across their organizations.

One more, from TechCrunch AI: SpaceX has secured a binding commitment for roughly $40 billion in financing for AI chips and data center equipment through an entity managed by Apollo Global Management. Apollo’s entity will buy the hardware and lease it to SpaceX for about five to five-and-a-quarter years after each delivery. SpaceX expects the transaction to close by year-end, subject to closing conditions, with deliveries and leases beginning in the first half of 2027. Apollo said the financing will support Nvidia Vera Rubin chips for SpaceX’s terrestrial computing expansion. SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen said the agreement diversifies funding sources and claimed the company’s AI infrastructure investments are generating payback periods of less than one year.

Another update, from OpenAI Blog: CodeRabbit is using GPT‑6.1 Sol as the primary model in its security agent to investigate vulnerabilities across code changes, repositories and linked components. The system provides review independent of the code-writing agent and complements established scanners with other frontier models and security tools. GPT‑6.1 Sol supports pull-request reviews and broader Deep Scan investigations into dependencies and component interactions. CodeRabbit Secure extends investigations to linked repositories, helping teams assess how weaknesses could affect a wider system. CodeRabbit reports that the model has flagged security-related issues across thousands of repositories and organizations.

Looking ahead, from r/MachineLearning: Deepity’s developer announced an initial public release of a C++20 predictive coding library with Python bindings, seeking feedback and contributors. In developer-reported MNIST tests on one i7-12700H laptop CPU with matched configurations, Deepity achieved 98.21% accuracy in 487.5 seconds, versus jpc’s 97.55% in 531.4 seconds and ngc-learn’s 98% in 1707.7 seconds. Version 0.1.0a3 is solo-maintained alpha software: the CPU path is benchmarked, while CUDA large-scale training optimization remains unfinished and no validated GPU comparison is supplied.

Finally, from MIT News AI: MIT brought a cross-campus coalition to Climate Week NYC, held Sept. 21–27, 2026, organizing 14 sessions through the MIT Climate Project and eight departments, labs, centers, and initiatives. MIT events at Manhattan’s Dream Downtown Hotel attracted more than 700 attendees and covered sustainable data centers, super pollutants, flood resilience, food security, industrial decarbonization, and climate education. Evelyn Wang, MIT’s vice president for energy and climate, emphasized that climate solutions require collaboration across institutions and sectors. Gang Chen, an MIT engineering professor and Climate Project faculty director, led a discussion on technologies that could help data centers address interconnected technical, economic, operational, and community constraints.
