### Daniel Ek backs AI researcher’s fusion power startup. Here’s what they’re building.

Spotify co-founder Daniel Ek has invested in Europa Fusion, a United Kingdom-based fusion power startup that operates in stealth mode. The company was established in February by Nantas Nardelli, an artificial intelligence researcher who previously held leadership roles at Google DeepMind, Facebook AI Research, and Microsoft Research Cambridge. Nardelli most recently served as the head of machine learning at Pacific Fusion, a Fremont, California, firm that has recently begun construction on a demonstration facility in New Mexico. Nardelli departed Pacific Fusion because he disagreed with the industry standard of relying heavily on physical hardware testing to refine simulations. He believes fusion developers should instead prioritize a simulation- and data-heavy methodology. Despite this strategic difference, Europa Fusion will utilize the same pulsed-power approach as Pacific Fusion, which employs timed electrical surges to compress fuel targets until atomic fusion occurs. Fusion technology offers the potential for carbon-free energy derived from nearly limitless fuel supplies, which could help mitigate the growing energy demands of artificial intelligence. Nardelli stated that his transition from AI research to fusion was motivated by a desire to solve climate change permanently. No fusion power plant has yet achieved commercial viability or grid connectivity, but successful development could provide a significant clean energy source in the coming decades. ### YouTube is reportedly preparing to settle social media addiction claims for $3 billion

YouTube is preparing to pay $3 billion to thousands of school districts to settle claims that its platform design contributed to social media addiction among young users. Bloomberg reports that the company reached the agreement in principle, which also mandates the implementation of new safeguards for teenagers. This financial resolution allows Google to avoid litigation from US school districts that accuse YouTube, along with Meta, Snap, and TikTok, of negatively impacting teen mental health. A previous bellwether trial involving these four companies concluded with a $27 million settlement before proceeding to court in Kentucky. The next scheduled trial is set for February 2027 in Oakland, California, and will feature Meta, Snap, and TikTok as defendants. Meta previously agreed to pay $17.1 billion over ten years to settle lawsuits brought by 47 states and various districts. That agreement includes stricter teen protections and conditions the release of the final $5.3 billion on YouTube and TikTok implementing similar changes. Both YouTube and Meta were found negligent in a separate legal battle regarding social media addiction earlier this year. Google did not immediately respond to requests for comment regarding the latest settlement terms. ([YouTube is reportedly preparing to settle social media addiction claims for $3 billion](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1039096/youtube-social-media-addiction-school-districts-settlement)).

### Google Workers Tell Their Employer Not to Punish Schools That Don't Want AI

Hundreds of Google employees are urging senior leadership to retract a controversial policy that would deny schools using its Gemini AI tools access to Google Search starting next year. The company announced the decision on Election Day, stating the move was intended to address concerns from teachers and parents regarding artificial intelligence in classrooms. Since the announcement, protests have erupted in multiple cities including Los Angeles, Chicago, Boston, Portland, Boulder, and Durham. Participants at a rally in New York City held signs opposing AI in schools and received support from passing motorists. These demonstrations were organized by a nationwide alliance of parent and community advocacy groups. Many school districts rely on Google software and Chromebooks, which became popular during the pandemic as affordable devices for remote learning. The Chromebooks feature built-in AI tools, and Google Search integrates AI Overview and AI Mode powered by Gemini. In response to the backlash, the school districts of New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago are now discussing alternatives to the current search engine. The Alphabet Workers Union presented a petition to CEO Sundar Pichai and other executives, arguing that the policy would likely drive schools to shut out Google products entirely. The petition emphasized that educators need the freedom to choose how they implement AI in their curriculums. A software engineer in Google’s education department stated that most workers were unaware of the search decision before it was publicly announced. Corporate leadership has acknowledged the petition but has not addressed its specific demands. Employees expressed concern that management prioritizes the company's bottom line over the best interests of students. One engineer questioned why the company would not trust users to adopt the technology on their own terms if they truly believed in its value. Google has not yet provided a comment on the matter. ### Welcome Inkling 2 Mini, Cutting-edge Performance for your Local Setup

Thinking Machines Lab has released Inkling 2 Mini, a multimodal Mixture-of-Experts model designed for local AI setups. The model features approximately 26 billion physical total parameters and activates only 3 billion parameters per token. It supports a context size of up to 1 million tokens and accepts both image and text inputs. The architecture utilizes a looping mechanism where the 12 middle layers of its 24 decoder layers execute three times per token. This design allows the model to behave as if it possesses 48 logical layers while maintaining only 24 physical ones. Each pass through the looped core reuses the same weights and refines information via cross-pass messages. The model routes tokens to eight experts selected from 128 available options per layer. Thinking Machines Lab ships the model in a mixed-precision format using NVFP4 and MXFP8 expert weights. A DSpark drafter is included to provide additional speedup with a minimal memory cost. The release also includes a research variant that supports a variable number of loop passes. This research artifact allows users to reduce passes to two or one to increase inference speed. Thinking Machines Lab provides support for Transformers, Llama App, and MLX. SGLang offers day-zero inference support for production deployments on datacenter hardware. Inkling 2 Mini ranks at the top of its class in the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5. It leads in 32 out of 48 benchmarks compared to models like Gemma 4 31B and Qwen3.8-27B. The model demonstrates strong capabilities in coding, reasoning, and vision tasks. Users can run the model on macOS and Windows using Llama App. Linux users can serve the model via the command line using llama.cpp. SGLang supports speculative decoding with the DSpark draft algorithm to improve generation speed. MLX inference works with the original checkpoint using native quantization. Thinking Machines Lab conducted vibe tests that compared the model favorably to Qwen 3.8 27B. The model successfully generated complex WebGL shaders and functional web applications from text and image prompts. Adversarial distillation enables an adversary to train a new model on the outputs or protected reasoning of an existing model, potentially creating a tool that can perform dangerous tasks, such as cyberattacks, that the original model would have refused. To combat this threat, OpenAI has updated the safeguards in its GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol models, focusing heavily on preventing the extraction of protected reasoning traces, which are the intermediate steps a model generates internally to reach an answer. The company reports that these updated safeguards significantly reduced the average attack success rate for recovering protected reasoning from 54.0 percent in the previous GPT-6 Astra model to 0.2 percent in GPT-6.2 Astra. This improvement is part of a broader defense strategy that combines adversarial training, classifier safeguards, and API-level protections. At the individual request level, OpenAI uses input and output classifiers to detect attempts to extract reasoning and prevent leaks in both final answers and tool-call arguments. The company also ties protected reasoning to the specific conversation history that produced it, ensuring that older model families with weaker safeguards cannot access the reasoning of newer models. At the campaign level, OpenAI investigates coordinated attempts to extract model capabilities and enforces against networks of accounts involved in unauthorized distillation. The company shares indicators of compromise with other model providers and government entities to help disrupt these campaigns across the industry. OpenAI has identified several unauthorized distillation campaigns this year, with enforcement actions ranging from thousands to tens of thousands of accounts. The company has also highlighted national security implications, noting that foreign actors may attempt to distill U.S. models to bypass safety controls. - [Q&A: Enabling more human-like interactions and understanding in multimodal foundation models](https://news.mit.edu/2026/qa-enabling-more-human-interactions-understanding-multimodal-foundation-models-1120): Jim Glass, a senior research scientist in the MIT Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, co-led an MIT-IBM Computing Research Lab investigation into the performance gaps of state-of-the-art multimodal artificial intelligence models. The research team focused on overcoming these limitations through data-efficient methods and novel benchmarks rather than simply scaling up model sizes. Glass explained that the project aimed to explore the relationship between perception and language without requiring large amounts of annotated training data. The team developed techniques to help models re-emerge latent capabilities that might disappear during fine-tuning processes. Early iterations of the work involved teaching models to learn automatic correspondences between audio captions and images. Researchers then expanded this approach to include general audio streams and video sequences. The team utilized methods where models reconstructed masked images or audio to learn strong relationships between different sensory inputs. As large language models became available, the research shifted toward understanding their limitations in audio and visual contexts. Recent highlights of this work include the IPLoc technique, which uses pseudonym substitution to force models to pay closer attention to visual context for object localization. Another study, OmniR1, revealed that some audio-large language models were gaming multiple-choice benchmarks by ignoring the actual audio input. This finding led the team to develop evaluation methods that require models to pay more attention to the audio data. The researchers utilized weakly supervised training techniques, such as test-time reinforcement learning, to adapt existing foundation models without extensive resources. The MIT-IBM collaboration produced numerous significant papers that demonstrated machines could learn correspondences without explicit instruction. The team plans to shift its focus toward full-duplex spoken dialogue systems that allow for natural, simultaneous listening and talking. These new systems aim to overcome the rigid turn-taking approaches currently used in many existing models. Rogerio Feris, Brian Kingsbury, and Samuel Thomas from IBM Research, along with Hilde Kuehne from the University of Tuebingen, collaborated on this research.

Buckmaster published a 79-page paper titled Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations on November 20, 2026. The repository hosts this paper alongside a Lean 4 formalization package that utilizes Mathlib version 4.33.1. A README file within the Lean directory details how the formal statements correspond to specific sections of the paper and highlights differences between the formal and written proofs. The formalization includes a large library of approximately 2,270 files and 600,000 lines, and building it requires significant computational resources and time. Statement files define theorems with placeholder proofs, while a solution file derives these results from the Breakdown library. Comparator JSON files link statement files to the solution and restrict the permitted axioms to propext, Quot.sound, and Classical.choice. The repository does not contain data or the certificate referenced in the paper, which relies on a separate rigorous computation using the Arb library through FLINT. The paper source code is also absent from this repository.
