## The News

The main reports are dated November 4, 2026.

AI releases emphasize practical cost and hardware gains, while Uber reports minimal agent usage and security stories raise verification questions.

First, from r/comfyui: A ComfyUI user reports that RefMods with MiniMax H3 maintained character appearance and voice across clips on a 6 GB VRAM laptop. They describe reusable references extracted from eight character images or a short voice clip, without retraining, and offer a video walkthrough and free workflow. The author presents this as solving their own consistency problem, not as independently verified results.

Next, from Hacker News: Layer3 Intel reported 236 residential proxy exits through six IP addresses on AS2650, the network registered to the Executive Office of the President that hosts the White House network, between October 5 and November 3, 2026. The company observed activity on 26 of the 30 days across 28 proxy services and said public IP context pages from Spur and Synthient independently identified the addresses as residential proxy exits. Layer3 Intel said its researchers encountered the network through routine global rotating proxy access rather than targeted probing. About half the activity occurred between 5 p.m. and midnight Eastern time, a pattern the company interpreted as potentially consistent with a person’s device running a proxy SDK.

Across 18 sampled comments: The substantive reaction mixes concern about covert proxy software with skepticism about the White House attribution. Commenters speculate about IoT devices and bundled software, while several ask how to detect proxy activity on their own networks.

Also, from r/LocalLLaMA: Ricursive Intelligence has introduced Ripple-65B Preview, an Apache 2.0-licensed dense Transformer with 65 billion parameters, pretrained on 12 trillion tokens and supporting context lengths of up to one million tokens. The release includes BF16 and NVFP4 instruction-tuned checkpoints and a BF16 base model. Ricursive reports a Terminal-Bench 2.1 score of 80.52, ahead of the listed results for Reflection Beam, Meta Muse Spark Open, Thinking Machines Inkling and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra, despite their much larger total parameter counts. Ripple also leads the listed comparison on SWE-bench Pro v2.0 Hard with 79.1, but competing models score higher on several other coding, knowledge and instruction-following benchmarks. Ricursive cautions that agentic benchmark results vary with evaluation settings and that its comparisons combine official scores with internal testing.

Across 10 sampled comments: Reaction is cautiously enthusiastic about another midsize dense model, but discussion centers on whether quantized versions and long-context workloads will fit local hardware.

Then, from Hacker News: LED outdoor lights attract fewer insects than incandescent and compact fluorescent bulbs, largely because typical LEDs do not emit ultraviolet light that attracts many insects. A 2016 University of Bristol field study found significantly fewer insects around LEDs, including fewer flies and Culicoides midges, but found no significant difference between cool-white and warm-white LEDs. Artificial light can disrupt flying insects’ orientation by prompting them to turn their backs toward the light, causing circling or inverted flight. Artificial lighting at night can also interfere with feeding and reproduction and is considered a contributor to insect population declines.

Across 18 sampled comments: The thread is largely skeptical of the categorical headline: several commenters report insects swarming LED porch lights, headlights, and farm equipment, while a few report fewer moths.

Worth noting, from TechCrunch AI: Uber said AI agents booked just four rides and placed 500 Uber Eats orders during Halloween week in October, compared with roughly 320 million trips and orders across both services. Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi described adoption as “very, very early” with limited traction. The figures contrast with growing enthusiasm for personal AI agents that perform tasks on users’ behalf. DoorDash similarly reported receiving only a few hundred AI-agent orders, with most customers continuing to use its app.

Another item, from TechCrunch AI: Meta has acquired Conductor, an AI coding startup that coordinates multiple agents in parallel workspaces to build software. Meta confirmed the acquisition but disclosed no deal terms. Conductor founder and CEO Charlie Holtz said the company plans to introduce a new interface by early next year. Conductor’s core features will continue operating as before until then, except for its cloud service, which will shut down on November 20 and begin deleting user cloud data afterward. Conductor was founded in 2024 and uses agents from providers including Anthropic and OpenAI rather than developing its own models. The acquisition follows Meta’s August launch of its Muse Code coding agent and other AI-related purchases, including the agent platform Moltbook.

Also in the mix, from Hacker News: Lewis cites YouTube adverts promising rapid physical transformations, universally adaptable prescription glasses, unlimited free internet from a USB stick and near-instant room cooling from a book-sized device. Lewis describes an unsolicited marketing email that echoed the language of his poetry collection’s description and claimed an early five-star rating. Lewis suspects that the sender used ChatGPT to generate the pitch without reading the collection, which he distributes as a free PDF. Lewis warns that scammers need only a small proportion of recipients to respond to make money, while personalized messages could make their pitches more persuasive. Lewis calls for platforms to verify products before accepting adverts but doubts that platforms will sacrifice advertising revenue.

Shifting gears, from Hugging Face Blog: Dharma-AI has introduced Field Extractor, a 2-billion-parameter model that converts document images into structured JSON for workflows such as invoice processing, medical scheduling and identity records. The model can identify fields without a predefined schema or populate an exact schema with typed values, nested tables and null values for missing information. Field Extractor builds on DharmaOCR-LITE v1.1 with a LoRA adapter trained on 43,384 examples spanning English, Brazilian Portuguese and other languages. The same checkpoint handles multiple document types without per-type fine-tuning.

One more, from Hacker News: PC Deal Check is a public beta service that evaluates computer asking prices for visitors in the United States using US retailer listings and prices. The service is free and requires no account or hardware expertise. Users can paste a retailer’s product link or upload a photo of the price and specifications. AI helps identify the main components, while a custom pricing engine compares the asking price with market-based component estimates to assign a deal grade.

Another update, from The Verge: Qualcomm expects data center chips to eventually account for about 30 percent of its business as smartphone sales fall roughly 20 percent from 2025. Qualcomm forecasts data center revenue of around $650 million in the first quarter of 2027, rising to $5 billion by the end of 2027 and $15 billion by 2029. Qualcomm’s AI data center expansion includes partnerships with Amazon and Meta announced earlier in 2026. AMD has set a higher eventual target, expecting data centers to generate more than 80 percent of its revenue.

Looking ahead, from r/artificial: Cognition announced SWE-2 Flash on November 4, 2026, positioning it as roughly 3x faster at one-fifth the price of SWE-2 for routine coding-agent work, rather than benchmark leadership. Available in Devin Desktop and CLI and free through November 30, it has 276B total parameters and 12B active, adapted from Thinking Machines Lab’s Inkling-Small. Cognition reports mixed results against SWE-2 and has not released the weights, citing cybersecurity concerns while developing a trusted-partner sharing program. The post asks about speed-versus-benchmark and weight-access tradeoffs; no resolution is supplied.

Finally, from r/StableDiffusion: Fizgig has added support for BRIA’s 8.2-billion-parameter Fibo 2.0 text-to-image and editing model, enabling LoRA, edit LoRA, slider LoRA and full fine-tune training on graphics cards with as little as 12 GB of memory. Fibo 2.0’s weights are restricted to non-commercial use. Fizgig supports masked training, four-step previews using Comfy-Org’s Turbo LoRA, Studio, LoRA Royale, Profiler and Extract for Fibo 2.0. Every Fibo file saved by Fizgig, including full fine-tunes, loads directly in ComfyUI. Fizgig’s setup downloads approximately 28 GB of models from Comfy-Org without requiring a Hugging Face token.
