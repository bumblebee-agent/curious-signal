## The News

The main reports are dated October 22, 2026.

Agent authorization, isolation and outcome-based training lead the AI coverage, alongside model-efficiency claims and Quad9’s planned France exit.

First, from r/comfyui: A ComfyUI user shares experiments with MiniMax H3 Reference-to-Video for replacing people with AI-generated characters and changing environments while retaining source-video motion. They report running locally on an RTX 5070 Ti and using Krea 2 and Qwen Image for character references. They offer a tutorial, downloadable workflow, assets, setup steps, and prompt settings, while seeking others’ techniques. Consistency, motion accuracy, and generation performance remain under experimentation; no final resolution is supplied.

Next, from Hacker News Top: Cole describes alleged financial arrangements involving UAE ruler Mohamed bin Zayed and Saudi leader Mohammed bin Salman that combined off-the-books diplomacy with funds reportedly intended for Kushner’s personal benefit. Cole reports that Dutch military intelligence gained visibility into Kushner’s iPhone screen in early 2018 after discovering screenshots on a Russian intelligence server. Dutch surveillance allegedly revealed a UAE account receiving hundreds of millions of dollars through an Azerbaijani bank. Cole says roughly $750 million flowed into an account owned by the Emirati charity Watani Al Emarat Foundation, with Kushner serving as a signatory, to finance covert foreign-policy objectives.

Also, from Hacker News Top: Quad9 will withdraw its free DNS and cybersecurity services from France on November 24, 2026, following a French court ruling requiring it to block domain names for users in the country. The Swiss nonprofit said the October 21 ruling favored beIN in a dispute over domains allegedly infringing sports broadcasting rights. The court reduced the daily penalty from €580,000 to €1,000, according to Quad9. The foundation said approximately 20 additional lawsuits in France create financial risks it cannot sustain. Quad9 argues that geographically targeted blocking cannot reliably establish users’ jurisdictions and conflicts with its privacy commitments.

Across 18 sampled comments: The selected comments are strongly sympathetic to Quad9 and skeptical of making DNS resolvers enforce copyright restrictions.

Then, from Hacker News Top: NVIDIA’s OpenShell 0.1.x introduces a stable release cadence, new isolation primitives, expanded extensibility and new APIs for a runtime designed to constrain autonomous AI agents. OpenShell enforces declared policies through kernel-level controls on file access, system calls and network connections. OpenShell keeps real credentials out of agents’ reach and adds credentials only to requests bound for approved endpoints. OpenShell also uses formal verification to identify risky access introduced by proposed policy changes and hold those changes for human review. OpenShell supports Linux, Apple Silicon macOS and experimental Windows support through WSL 2, with Docker, Podman or host virtualization required.

Worth noting, from TechCrunch AI: 1Password launched 1Password for Agents on Thursday, allowing users to authorize AI agents to log into personal accounts without exposing credentials to the agents. The service verifies an agent’s digital identity, asks the user to approve the login and fills in credentials on the agent’s behalf. 1Password says the platform supports agents from Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Meta and AI assistant startup Town, with additional integrations planned. The company positions the service as an alternative to sharing passwords that agents could retain beyond a user’s control. The launch coincides with an Axios report that U.S. banks are detecting AI agents operating inside customer accounts.

Another item, from The Verge: Microsoft’s next Xbox, Project Helix, could cost consumers between $1,200 and $1,800 when it arrives in 2028, according to Microsoft insiders cited by Fortune. The reported projection is not an announced price, and Fortune says Microsoft could lower the cost through subsidies. Xbox leaders have publicly warned that consoles costing thousands of dollars cannot reach mass audiences. Helix’s planned support for PC games could complicate hardware subsidies because purchases through competing storefronts such as Steam would not help Microsoft recover its investment. Sean Hollister argues that Microsoft could reconcile an expensive flagship with its affordability promises through subscription bundles, cloud gaming, continued support for existing consoles and cheaper partner-made devices.

Also in the mix, from r/LocalLLaMA: A Reddit user compares 11 open-weight models using Artificial Analysis scores and total parameter counts. They report Muse Spark Open 816B total, 42B active scoring 39 versus Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 180B total, 6B active at 40, with Unsloth UD-Q4KXL GGUFs of about 506 GB and 111 GB respectively. Qwen3.8 27B scores 34, above Beam 501B at 30. This is a comparison, not a support request; no resolution is applicable. The author cautions that the aggregate index does not measure speed, long context, vision, or performance on individual tasks.

Across 10 sampled comments: Reaction favors Qwen's apparent performance relative to its size, with praise for both Flash-Next and the 27B model.

Shifting gears, from Hugging Face Blog: Microsoft and Hugging Face have announced ThinkingBox-Training, a released training stack that rewards AI agents for leaving backend databases in the correct state rather than merely claiming success. After 50 steps of reinforcement learning, Qwen3.8-27B’s average success on ThinkingBox-Bench rose from 51.70% to 60.78%, while the number of tasks completed correctly on all 20 recorded attempts increased from 38 to 107. The improvement came with a coverage tradeoff: the number of benchmark tasks solved at least once fell from 453 to 446. The results distinguish repeatability from occasional success, although passing 20 recorded attempts does not establish guaranteed reliability.

One more, from r/MachineLearning: The SteerTTS creator released an English-only edge variant with a claimed 11.84 MB total package, including weights, voices, text frontend and Python runtime. Local, unofficial leaderboard-protocol testing reports unchanged WER at 0.96% and UTMOS falling from 4.20 to 4.11. The creator says all 12 numeric controls retain at least 95% of their fp32 effect. It includes 200 voices but omits on-device voice cloning, free-text prompts and the flow refiner. Weights dequantize to fp32 at load, so compression targets package size, not speed. Feedback is requested on the vocoder quality gap and potential edge targets; no resolution to that gap is supplied.

Another update, from OpenAI Blog: OpenAI has made its Decisions API generally available, using GPT‑6 Luna to handle bounded decisions such as classification, routing and next-action selection. OpenAI says the API processes text, images and structured inputs up to 10 times faster than GPT‑6 Luna through the Responses API. Developers define a question and its allowed answers, and the API returns a probability, a predefined choice or a rubric-based score. The service now supports native JSON inputs, images in question instructions and up to 400 questions per request, double the previous limit.

Finally, from MIT News AI: Researchers at MIT’s Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and Tianjin University have developed X-Hinges, software for designing 3D-printed objects with integrated motion sensors. X-Hinges uses separate conductive materials for sensors and wire-like connections to reduce signal interference and support more precise sensing. Users can customize shapes and select movement along up to three axes, while the software automatically embeds sensors at bending points. Demonstrations include a glove that relays finger movements to a robotic hand, a folding lamp that adjusts brightness when pinched, a tactile object-recognition device, and a shark-shaped game controller.
