## Summary
The video promotes a daily coffee mixture—**1 teaspoon of Ceylon cinnamon plus one scoop of grass-fed collagen peptides**, described as providing **10–20 g of protein**—as a way to reduce cravings, stabilize blood sugar, and support healthy aging, particularly after age 40.

### Main claims
- The presenter attributes morning jitters, hunger, afternoon crashes, and mitochondrial damage to caffeine on an empty stomach, compounded by sugar and flavored creamers.
- Coffee allegedly activates **AMPK**, a pathway associated with exercise, fasting, and mitochondrial biogenesis.
- Cinnamon allegedly improves insulin sensitivity and reduces glucose absorption; collagen allegedly slows gastric emptying, stimulates endogenous **GLP-1**, and supports skin, joints, and recovery.
- Suggested timing includes water first and delaying coffee **30 minutes, preferably 90 minutes**, particularly when stressed or sleep-deprived.

### Evidence and limitations
The presenter mentions a **2025 Cell and Bioscience study**, a **2023 meta-analysis of 24 controlled trials**, a **2024 collagen-peptide paper**, and a **2023 randomized trial** involving skin advanced glycation end products (**AGEs**). However, the transcript supplies no titles, authors, study populations, doses, or effect sizes sufficient to evaluate these claims.

Research on individual ingredients or cellular pathways does **not establish that this exact coffee mixture delivers the promised outcomes**. The transcript provides no direct trial of the combined protocol. Claims of predictable improvements within minutes through months—including lower A1C, blood pressure, belly fat, and reversed aging damage—are presented largely through assertions and anecdotes.

### Commercial context and cautions
- The video becomes an **Equip product promotion**, advertising collagen, “clean coffee,” additional protein powder, an exclusive **20% discount**, and a money-back guarantee.
- Third-party testing is claimed, but the report is not included.
- Assertions that ordinary coffee is broadly mold-contaminated, artificial sweeteners universally damage gut health, or collagen-driven GLP-1 release is comparable to weight-loss medication are not substantiated in the supplied material.
- “No side effects” and “no risk” are promotional absolutes, not demonstrated safety findings.

**Bottom line:** A supplement sales pitch built around biological mechanisms and incompletely identified research—not demonstrated evidence that cinnamon-and-collagen coffee prevents glucose crashes, treats insulin resistance, or reverses aging. A useful read-only follow-up would be checking the cited studies’ populations, formulations, outcomes, and commercial conflicts.
