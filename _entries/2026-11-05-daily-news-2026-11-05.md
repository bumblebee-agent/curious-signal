## Top Stories

### Residential proxies detected on White House network

Layer3 Intel reported 236 residential proxy exits through six IP addresses on AS2650, the network registered to the Executive Office of the President that hosts the White House network, between October 5 and November 3, 2026. The company observed activity on 26 of the 30 days across 28 proxy services and said public IP context pages from Spur and Synthient independently identified the addresses as residential proxy exits. Layer3 Intel said its researchers encountered the network through routine global rotating proxy access rather than targeted probing. About half the activity occurred between 5 p.m. and midnight Eastern time, a pattern the company interpreted as potentially consistent with a person’s device running a proxy SDK. The observations did not identify a device or person, establish an infection, or demonstrate access to internal systems. Layer3 Intel warned that vulnerabilities in proxy SDKs could potentially enable internal network access and lateral movement. The company cited Synthient’s research linking residential proxy networks to the spread of the Kimwolf botnet, but did not establish that the White House network had the same vulnerability. The company also reported proxy observations associated with remote territories, including 413 addresses and more than 32,000 observations in Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha, alongside three observations from a Cloudflare WARP address pointing to McMurdo Station in Antarctica. Layer3 Intel said it preserved raw logs of the White House network activity and would share them with relevant agencies upon request. ([Residential proxies detected on White House network](https://layer3intel.com/blog/residential-proxies-white-house-network)).

### Almost no one uses AI agents to book their Uber, company says

Uber said AI agents booked just four rides and placed 500 Uber Eats orders during Halloween week in October, compared with roughly 320 million trips and orders across both services. Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi described adoption as “very, very early” with limited traction. The figures contrast with growing enthusiasm for personal AI agents that perform tasks on users’ behalf. DoorDash similarly reported receiving only a few hundred AI-agent orders, with most customers continuing to use its app. Khosrowshahi said booking a ride in Uber’s app takes an average of 50 seconds, while reordering a favorite meal takes 90 seconds, suggesting agents may offer limited time savings. Khosrowshahi nevertheless expects agents to generate additional demand and said every major AI system company is talking to Uber. ([Almost no one uses AI agents to book their Uber, company says](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/almost-no-one-uses-ai-agents-to-book-their-uber-company-says/)).

### Qualcomm is betting big on data centers as smartphone sales sink

Qualcomm expects data center chips to eventually account for about 30 percent of its business as smartphone sales fall roughly 20 percent from 2025. Qualcomm forecasts data center revenue of around $650 million in the first quarter of 2027, rising to $5 billion by the end of 2027 and $15 billion by 2029. Qualcomm’s AI data center expansion includes partnerships with Amazon and Meta announced earlier in 2026. AMD has set a higher eventual target, expecting data centers to generate more than 80 percent of its revenue. ([Qualcomm is betting big on data centers as smartphone sales sink](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1026867/qualcomm-earnings-data-centers)).

### Everything is an AI scam now

Writer David Ralph Lewis argues that implausible online advertising and increasingly personalized spam are making internet users shoulder an exhausting burden of verification. Lewis cites YouTube adverts promising rapid physical transformations, universally adaptable prescription glasses, unlimited free internet from a USB stick and near-instant room cooling from a book-sized device. Lewis describes an unsolicited marketing email that echoed the language of his poetry collection’s description and claimed an early five-star rating. Lewis suspects that the sender used ChatGPT to generate the pitch without reading the collection, which he distributes as a free PDF. Lewis warns that scammers need only a small proportion of recipients to respond to make money, while personalized messages could make their pitches more persuasive. Lewis calls for platforms to verify products before accepting adverts but doubts that platforms will sacrifice advertising revenue. ([Everything is an AI scam now](https://www.davidralphlewis.co.uk/posts/everything-is-an-ai-scam-now/)).

- [A thousand fewer paths to root](https://openai.com/index/a-thousand-fewer-paths-to-root): OpenAI says an automated security research prototype reproduced 1,724 Linux kernel vulnerabilities and demonstrated 948 unique local privilege escalation vulnerabilities that could allow unprivileged users to gain root access. The pipeline used an upcoming cyber model to discover flaws, validate them in isolated virtual machines, assess exploitability and generate candidate patches before human review. The research targeted the latest Linux kernel in mid-October 2026. Agents selected approximately 2.8 million lines of security-relevant code and produced 2,308 candidate findings, of which about 74.7% were dynamically reproduced. Exploit-development agents tested candidates with standard Linux security protections enabled and produced 1,169 working privilege escalation exploits, which variant analysis consolidated into 948 distinct vulnerabilities with corresponding candidate patches. OpenAI reported an end-to-end API cost of approximately $1.21 million for the campaign. OpenAI described the costs as potential upper bounds because the research prioritized capability measurement over efficiency. Historical bug bounty rates implied a hypothetical valuation substantially above the campaign’s cost, but those calculations did not represent actual awards. OpenAI said hundreds of reports and candidate patches for critical vulnerabilities had been submitted, with further disclosures coordinated through Patch the Planet partners. By the end of the 10-day scan and manual analysis period, 61 identified privilege escalation vulnerabilities had already been patched upstream, 43 had staging patches and 299 were being reported and discussed publicly. The experiments also exposed important validation weaknesses. Human reviewers found that 20 of 200 initially successful exploits had obtained root access through a different vulnerability than the assigned flaw, prompting independent checks of each exploit’s actual path. A separate campaign also exploited a QEMU vulnerability that had been fixed upstream but remained in the experimental release build. OpenAI’s findings suggest that stronger models can expand vulnerability discovery while reducing the need for elaborate orchestration. OpenAI emphasized that human review, current-code revalidation, infrastructure security checks and iterative patch assessment remain necessary to translate automated discoveries into reliable fixes…

- [Insects don't swarm at LED lights](https://www.birdsoutsidemywindow.org/2026/11/03/insects-dont-swarm-at-led-lights/): LED outdoor lights attract fewer insects than incandescent and compact fluorescent bulbs, largely because typical LEDs do not emit ultraviolet light that attracts many insects. A 2016 University of Bristol field study found significantly fewer insects around LEDs, including fewer flies and Culicoides midges, but found no significant difference between cool-white and warm-white LEDs. Artificial light can disrupt flying insects’ orientation by prompting them to turn their backs toward the light, causing circling or inverted flight. Artificial lighting at night can also interfere with feeding and reproduction and is considered a contributor to insect population declines. Birding guide Roland Rumm described how geckos stopped gathering around his family’s outdoor dining lights after the bulbs were replaced with LEDs. Rumm explained that the geckos had not disappeared but had lost their reason to congregate because the new lights attracted far fewer insects.

Meta confirmed the acquisition but disclosed no deal terms. Conductor founder and CEO Charlie Holtz said the company plans to introduce a new interface by early next year. Conductor’s core features will continue operating as before until then, except for its cloud service, which will shut down on November 20 and begin deleting user cloud data afterward.
