### Sharing AI Progress in Mathematics

OpenAI is releasing a broad range of new mathematical results produced by an internal frontier model. The company consulted with the independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence at the Institute for Advanced Study to develop best practices for sharing these findings with the math community. OpenAI drew on the group’s advice and public recommendations to inform how it released the results. The organization is publishing the results in a GitHub repository that includes protocols for paper revisions and citations. OpenAI is continuing to explore other community-hosted alternatives that meet the committee’s guidelines. The company is committed to improving the quality of the papers through better citations, mathematical exposition, and presentation of the results. As part of the repository, OpenAI is sharing formalizations of many proofs in Lean, a programming language that allows mathematical proofs to be checked by a computer. The company will update the repository with more formalizations as it obtains them. To promote scientific transparency and openness, OpenAI is publishing additional details about how it obtained the results. These details include ten summaries of the model’s reasoning, estimations of compute spent in terms of Pro usage on ChatGPT, and statistics about the number of attempted problems. The average result used the equivalent compute of roughly three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking. OpenAI wants this progress to push the frontier of human knowledge and enable further progress in mathematics. The company will fund a series of workshops, conferences, and special programs around the understanding of major results produced by AI. OpenAI is working to responsibly release the model that produced these results to directly empower scientists with state-of-the-art capabilities. The company is evaluating its internal frontier models on mathematics and other sciences to accelerate developing tools to advance those fields. OpenAI will continue to act on feedback from the community and update its standards for future disclosures of major scientific advancements. ([Sharing AI Progress in Mathematics](https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/)).

Despite these efforts, many companies remain resistant to allowing third-party agents on their platforms. Delta Air Lines stated it has no current partnership with third-party AI agents and is evaluating how such technology might impact security and customer experience. ### Falcon-Emirati: When an LLM Learns the Dialect, the Culture, and the Nuance

The Technology Innovation Institute has released Falcon-Emirati-7B, a large language model specialized in understanding and generating Emirati Arabic. This model builds upon the Falcon-H1-Arabic foundation, which utilizes a hybrid architecture combining State Space Models and Transformer attention to handle long sequences and complex linguistic dependencies. The developers selected the seven-billion-parameter variant to balance the need for cultural nuance with practical training and inference costs. Emirati Arabic presents unique challenges for artificial intelligence because it is primarily a spoken dialect with limited written records online. Meaning in this dialect often relies on non-literal expressions, proverbs, and shared cultural context rather than surface-level vocabulary. To address these difficulties, the team constructed a data pipeline using authentic Emirati web content, Modern Standard Arabic materials focused on local heritage, and synthetic data constrained by specific glossaries and style rules. The institute evaluated the model using Alyah, a benchmark of 1,173 multiple-choice questions designed to test dialectal and cultural competence. Falcon-Emirati-7B achieved an accuracy score of 84.83 percent on this benchmark, outperforming other Arabic and multilingual models. The team also conducted open-ended generation tests judged by an LLM to assess dialect fidelity. Falcon-Emirati-7B demonstrated significantly higher dialect fidelity than competitors, reliably responding in Emirati Arabic rather than defaulting to Modern Standard Arabic. Further testing on the ArabCulture-Dialogue benchmark showed the model scored 85.57 percent on culturally appropriate responses in UAE scenarios. This performance placed it ahead of models such as ALLaM-7B, Jais-2-8B, and Fanar-2-27B. The results indicate that dialect competence requires targeted training rather than relying solely on model scale. The institute notes that the model may still reflect biases in its training data and advises users to evaluate it for specific use cases before deployment. ([Falcon-Emirati: When an LLM Learns the Dialect, the Culture, and the Nuance](https://huggingface.co/blog/tiiuae/falcon-emirati)).

The survey categorizes hardware into central processing units, graphics processing units, application-specific integrated circuits, field-programmable gate arrays, and dataflow accelerators. Each technology type offers distinct capabilities, with central processing units serving general-purpose computing and application-specific integrated circuits handling highly specific tasks. The LAICS team compares peak performance and power consumption across more than 120 accelerators in their latest report, an increase from the 57 devices examined in the first study. Researchers gather data from public sources, although they face challenges when companies keep performance metrics private. Reuther monitors daily news and citation searches to identify new technical announcements and industry presentations. The survey series explores various aspects of the field, including the impact of smaller transistor designs and lower numerical precision on performance. The most recent paper examines architectural choices such as core density and parallel performance capabilities. Reuther notes that the market remains dynamic, with new startups frequently introducing innovative accelerators despite perceptions of saturation. The center collaborates with other Lincoln Laboratory divisions to understand how these technologies support specific research and development missions. The survey results help government sponsors and laboratory users make informed decisions about technology acquisition and system purchases. The complete set of papers and accompanying datasets are available on GitHub.
