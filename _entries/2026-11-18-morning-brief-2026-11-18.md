## The News

The main reports are dated November 17, 2026.

AI launches target professional workflows while infrastructure competition, synthetic media and education concerns broaden the agenda.

First, from Hacker News: jQuery 4.1.0 and 3.8.0 have been released with changes covering Ajax, selectors, form serialization and browser compatibility. The selector updates fix a regular-expression denial-of-service issue in identifier matching, address a Safari 27 regex interpreter bug and improve handling of invalid escape code points. The updates add a responseURL field to jqXHR, support an alternative transport completion callback API and enable serialization of form-associated custom elements. Core changes replace getElementsByTagName and getElementsByClassName with querySelectorAll, while CSS fixes improve XML document support and reusable table-style probes. The project also expanded test coverage, added guidelines for machine-generated contributions and listed TuxCare as a commercial support vendor.

Across 18 sampled comments: The reaction is nostalgic but skeptical about jQuery's role in new projects: commenters credit it with making cross-browser JavaScript practical, while arguing that modern browser APIs cover many former use cases.

Next, from Hacker News: OpenAI has introduced GPT‑6.2 Astra for complex professional work and GPT‑6.2 Sol for everyday tasks, with both models available in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API. The company says both models improve performance while using fewer output tokens, lowering costs per task. OpenAI positions Astra as its strongest model for scientific work, professional documents, complex coding and interactive design. Astra scored 5.2 points above GPT‑6 Astra on Agents’ Last Exam at less than one-third the cost. On OpenAI’s internal GDPval v2 benchmark, Astra improved by 6.2 percentage points while using 22% fewer output tokens.

Across 18 sampled comments: Reaction is mixed: commenters see promising price-performance and game-generation demos, but want evidence from everyday work rather than release benchmarks or visual gimmicks.

Also, from Hacker News: RunAnywhere’s TrnRT is an open-source inference engine that serves GLM-5.3-Flash on a single AWS Trainium2 trn2.48xlarge instance through an OpenAI-compatible API. GLM-5.3-Flash is the first supported model, with 320 billion total parameters, 18 billion active parameters, FP8 weights and a one-million-token context window. TrnRT combines fused NKI decode kernels, device-and-host prefix caching and two inference engines behind a session-affine router. The API supports streaming, tool calls and reasoning output. Deployment requires Docker and offline compilation on an x86_64 host, which takes hours; the serving instance loads the compiled graphs without compiling by default.

Across 5 sampled comments: The small thread welcomes an open-source effort to make AWS Trainium more useful, while questioning its competitiveness and hardware coverage.

Then, from The Verge: Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang promoted the company’s consumer AI agent, Muse, with an AI-generated video featuring himself, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Muse mascot Jolly. The clip depicts Altman dressed as Jolly and jokes about OpenAI’s product strategy. Wang’s post highlights the growing normalization of synthetic portrayals of real people, even as Instagram faces criticism over unauthorized celebrity imagery. Actress Mia Goth’s representative criticized Instagram after an AI-generated image of Goth spread in October through a fan account carrying a blue verification badge.

Worth noting, from TechCrunch AI: London-based Model ML has raised $250 million in Series B funding at a $1.5 billion valuation to automate financial modeling, pitch decks and due diligence reports for bankers and consultants. General Atlantic and existing investor QED Investors co-led the round, which follows a $75 million Series A announced a year earlier. Brothers Chaz and Arnie Englander co-founded Model ML, whose AI agents integrate with clients’ systems and tools, including Excel and PowerPoint. Model ML says its agents understand deal context and automatically update financial models and presentations as developments change the analysis.

Another item, from TechCrunch AI: Nvidia’s board does not intend to reinstate founder and former CEO Jensen Huang, chairman Harvey C. Jones said during Tuesday’s earnings call. Jones said the board unanimously accepted Huang’s November 12 resignation after disclosures of personal behavior inconsistent with company standards. Huang had publicly offered to return if the board considered his reinstatement beneficial. Huang’s departure followed revelations of extramarital affairs, including a relationship with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom U.S. intelligence officials suspect of working for China’s Ministry of State Security. Nvidia’s board has launched an independent investigation into Jensen Huang’s activities, and the Securities and Exchange Commission is probing the company.

Also in the mix, from Hacker News: The notice directed visitors to contact the site owner and provide request ID 1474864105385556524. The website uses Tencent Cloud EdgeOne protection.

Shifting gears, from Latent Space: Cloudflare is expanding from AI infrastructure and model hosting into developing its own models, starting with Clef and Clairic. Michelle Chen, who leads product for Cloudflare’s AI Platform, said the company is also experimenting with reasoning models and other large language models, with an emphasis on post-training existing models rather than pretraining from scratch. Clef is a System One decision model inspired by TypeSafe AI’s Jev. Chen said Cloudflare built Clef in two weeks, crediting TypeSafe’s two years of groundwork.

One more, from MIT News AI: MIT’s recent report on artificial intelligence and education found that students are attending office hours less often and spending less time working through problem sets and collaborating with peers. The report warned that AI is eroding the “creative friction” of debate and intellectual challenge that has long shaped an MIT education. The MIT Human Insight Collaborative, known as MITHIC, is supporting interdisciplinary research into how AI affects learning, cognition, creativity, democracy, and human relationships. The initiative launched in 2024 and has awarded $5.5 million to 67 projects across 31 MIT units, with roughly one-third focused on AI, society, and humanity.

Finally, from OpenAI Blog: OpenAI has introduced ChatGPT for Sales, combining a new Sales plugin with more than 30 new and enhanced partner plugins to help sellers research accounts, prepare calls, tailor proposals and follow up with buyers. Supported workflows include prioritizing accounts, creating presentations, drafting outreach, identifying pipeline risks and preparing quotes and agreements. Partner tools include Salesforce, HubSpot, Gong, Clay, Canva and Docusign. OpenAI’s always-on agent, called a dot, can use connected plugins to prepare meeting briefs, track deal updates and surface proposed next steps for review.
