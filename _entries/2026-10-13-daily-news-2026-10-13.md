## Top Stories

### AI Is a Tool. Using It Well Takes Skill.

Rich Hickey argues in his October 11, 2026 article, “‘AI’ is Not a Tool,” that large language models are automation rather than tools because humans cannot wield them. A counterargument holds that automation and tool use are compatible, as compilers and spreadsheets demonstrate: people direct these systems through inputs, evaluate results, and decide what happens next. Effective LLM use requires judgment, feedback, and expertise. A programmer can question a model’s explanation, inspect the underlying code, test proposed changes, and reject incorrect answers. Unpredictability and unreliability limit an LLM’s usefulness, but those shortcomings do not necessarily disqualify software from being a tool. Replacing a paper with an AI-generated summary can sacrifice opportunities to learn, while reading the paper and using an LLM to challenge an interpretation can support further inquiry. The distinction depends on the user’s actions rather than the software category. Anthropomorphic descriptions such as “go rogue” or “conspire” risk attributing human motives to software failures. Unexpected outputs and harmful actions are engineering problems that require oversight. The more practical distinction separates skilled, deliberate use from delegation without adequate understanding or supervision. ([AI Is a Tool. Using It Well Takes Skill.](https://yyhh.org/blog/2026/10/ai-is-a-tool/)).

### Meta releases its first frontier model, Muse Wonder

Meta’s Superintelligence Lab released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full on Monday, marking the company’s first frontier-model launch. The larger models target more demanding tasks, including coding, than the lightweight, low-latency Muse Spark. Meta’s testing places their benchmark performance alongside Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra. Muse Wonder costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens through the Meta Model API. Muse Wonder Full is available only to Muse Code subscribers and will not be offered through the API. Both models will power the Muse agent. Meta emphasized “active memory,” the models’ ability to retain and synthesize user information over extended periods. Meta reports that Muse Wonder outperforms Fable, Opus and Astra on MemoryBench, which tests continual learning and memory construction. Research scientist Kavya Kopparapu said the company focused on helping the models remember information important to users over days, weeks and months. The launch follows Scale AI founder Alexandr Wang’s restructuring of Meta’s AI efforts in August 2025. Meta says the models were trained at its Prometheus data center in central Ohio, which came online earlier this year. ([Meta releases its first frontier model, Muse Wonder](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/12/meta-releases-its-first-frontier-model-muse-wonder/)).

### How Anthropic’s Dynamic Workflows Coordinate Claude Agents

Anthropic’s dynamic workflows let Claude decide how to divide tasks and organize multiple agents, replacing the developer-configured coordinator and subagent roster previously required by Claude Managed Agents. Anthropic technical staff members CJ Avilla and Harrison Stall demonstrated the feature at AI Engineer New York 2026, presenting it as a way to tackle ambitious projects without making human operators manage every agent connection. A lead agent creates a plan, and Claude writes an orchestration program that launches agents, collects results, and manages information flow. Developers can supply preconfigured specialist subagents and inspect agent activity and handoff payloads through graphing tools in Claude Console. Workflow agents share a filesystem for exchanging information, but the API does not let developers directly instruct one subagent to message another. A stock-watchlist demonstration distributed individual tickers to agents with isolated contexts before a review phase examined their findings and produced a report. CJ Avilla said the multiagent approach cost substantially more than a single-agent approach but found more details. Harrison Stall said evaluations and conservative defaults help limit excessive agent creation, while greater parallelization generally requires explicit encouragement. Developers can set session budgets, but the running model does not know its available budget. Anthropic’s research-preview feature called dreaming reorganizes memory using existing stores and past session transcripts, merging duplicates, replacing stale or contradicted entries, and surfacing insights. CJ Avilla suggested dreaming could improve memory across repeated workflows, although automatic changes to future scheduled runs were not built into the demonstrated scanner. Harrison Stall described dynamic workflows as a way to apply additional test-time compute to difficult problems. Anthropic’s stated ambition is to make large-scale scientific work more accessible to general users, although the presentation did not establish how far the feature can deliver on that goal. ([How Anthropic’s Dynamic Workflows Coordinate Claude Agents](https://www.latent.space/p/claude-dynamic-workflows)).

Their forecast relies on a general capability index rather than a direct measure of replication ability, making the timeline uncertain. PerfectLab Studio composes prompts, image dimensions, seeds and metadata for sessions of up to 100 shots with one to four takes per shot. Each frame can rotate through outfits, crops, poses and expressions, while persona settings and a LoRA trigger line support character consistency. A fixed-seed option supports controlled comparisons, and individual frames can be re-shot with a fresh seed. The node’s dramaturgy arcs give sessions an emotional progression by adjusting expressions and lighting intensity, with optional changes to crops, poses, placement, focus and take counts. Per-frame metadata records arc levels and adjustments, and CSV exports provide captioning and calibration material. PerfectLab Studio offers natural-language prompts for Z-Image, Flux and Qwen-Image, alongside comma-separated tags for SDXL. Its prompt linter checks model-specific token budgets, duplicate wording, malformed choices and missing wildcard files. Users can add custom library entries, preview images, presets, wildcard lists and emotional curves, while the interface supports Russian and English and keeps generated prompts in English.

- [Linux founder Linus Torvalds says AI is a “gateway drug” into programming — but cautions about using AI for “serious” work](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/linux-founder-linus-torvalds-says-103004221.html): Linus Torvalds, the founder and key maintainer of the Linux kernel, described AI as a useful gateway into programming during the Open Source Summit in Prague, Czechia. Torvalds argued that AI can help beginners enjoy learning rather than feel discouraged by comparisons between their early efforts and polished professional software. Torvalds recalled that his early computer games were poor, but contemporary software set a low enough bar that he did not feel ashamed of his work. Torvalds encouraged beginners to experiment with AI on toy or personal projects, while warning that important work requires users to understand their goals and the outcomes they need. Torvalds also reiterated concerns about AI-generated vulnerability reports and patches overwhelming Linux maintainers. Torvalds said duplicate reports and findings involving long-unused code create substantial sorting work, even as some AI-generated patches address genuine security issues and improve the codebase. Torvalds said roughly three quarters of discussions at the preceding maintainer summit concerned making AI code generation and review less stressful and more useful. Torvalds described maintainers using AI-assisted patch reviews to counter the flood of AI-generated submissions, creating situations in which bots effectively talk to other bots.
- [Codex CLI installs a persistent, self-updating background daemon by default](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/48195): A Codex CLI user running version 0.157.0 on Linux through WSL2 is asking for automatic daemon startup to become opt-in or require one-time consent. The user reports that opening an interactive session starts a persistent background server, copies roughly 374 MB of installed binaries into a separate daemon directory, and launches an updater that checks for releases after five minutes and then hourly. The user says the daemon survives closed CLI windows, separates the backend version from the package-manager installation, and retains the proxy environment of the session that first launched it. The user also cites related reports of updates interrupting active turns and hooks receiving daemon-level working-directory or terminal context instead of session-specific context. The requested default would keep the backend tied to the CLI session and installed binary, while reserving a shared, self-updating daemon for desktop use, Remote Control, or explicit activation. Alternative safeguards would shut down an automatically started daemon after its last client disconnects and prevent copying or self-updating package-manager installations. The user reports that the daemon stop command leaves the detached updater running. Disabling automatic startup prevents new interactive sessions from spawning a daemon, according to the report.
- [WindBorne wins a hefty US Air Force contract for its balloon data](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/12/windborne-wins-a-hefty-us-air-force-contract-for-its-balloon-data/): The U.S. Air Force has awarded WindBorne Systems a contract worth up to $49 million for atmospheric data collected by its autonomous weather balloons. The agreement allows the Air Force to order data for specific regions and times rather than committing to the full amount, and the service has ordered $1.7 million worth so far. WindBorne operates about 700 balloons worldwide and uses their sensor readings both for customer data services and its own AI weather forecasting model. WindBorne co-founder and chief technology officer Andrey Sushko said the contract would support fleet expansion and research to improve balloon hardware and performance. Sushko said the government awarded the contract without competitive bidding because WindBorne alone could provide the global data capability in the form required for those operations. WindBorne raised a $37 million Series B round to expand its business and released its WeatherMesh 6 forecasting model earlier this summer. WindBorne says the model produces more accurate forecasts than the traditional physics-based model operated by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts, although both AI and traditional forecasting remain important for understanding weather. WindBorne became the first organization in August to send an unpowered flight system into a typhoon’s eye to collect data. The company steers its balloons by changing their altitude to reach winds moving in the desired direction, using air release or sand ballast drops. Sushko credited the achievement to accurate wind models, navigation capabilities and a sufficiently large balloon fleet.

- [Muse Wonder](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-wonder): Muse Wonder has launched as its developers’ largest and strongest model yet, with a focus on personal and professional agents that handle complex, ongoing tasks. The developers describe Muse Wonder as a frontier-class model designed to help with activities such as organizing a cross-country move, filing an insurance claim, or turning a hobby into a business. Muse Wonder’s training emphasizes real-world tasks that unfold over time and require tracking substantial information. Its Active Memory capability is designed to retain habits, preferences, and conversations over days, weeks, and months. Its Active Focus capability trains the model to persist through obstacles and try alternative solutions, while reproduced real-world failures help train the model to anticipate and avoid problems. The developers also emphasize everyday knowledge work in health, finance, and legal matters, including lease review.
