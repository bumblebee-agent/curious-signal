### Much shorter version of GRH Lean verification from Anthropic

An Anthropic research artifact claims Lean 4 proofs of the Riemann Hypothesis and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions. The proofs have passed machine checks, but no mathematician has read them through, and no accompanying paper is available. The repository credits work by Levent Alpöge with Claude and describes a shorter version produced with Anthropic colleagues and Claude. A readable edition contains about 300,000 lines, while a compact edition presents the same five theorems in about 76,000 lines. The projects build on Mathlib and report using only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. A build requires Lean v4.35.0 and a specified Mathlib commit, taking one to two hours on 12 cores with 24 GB of memory. The Apache 2.0-licensed repository is not maintained and does not accept contributions. ([Much shorter version of GRH Lean verification from Anthropic](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean)).

### S&P considers Nvidia credit downgrade over governance and ‘key man’ risks

S&P Global Ratings is considering cutting Nvidia’s credit rating from AA to AA- over governance concerns following the ouster and reinstatement of CEO Jensen Huang. The agency cited weak executive oversight, inadequate succession planning and heavy reliance on Huang despite Nvidia’s strong financial performance. S&P expects to complete its review within 90 days. Nvidia would retain an investment-grade rating, but a downgrade could increase its borrowing costs and complicate financing for AI infrastructure companies whose debt Nvidia backstops. Nvidia announced plans in August to arrange $500 billion in AI infrastructure financing with six financial institutions, initially guaranteeing 25% of the loans. Lenders had already sought larger guarantees before Huang’s departure and return, and a downgrade could intensify those demands. ([S&P considers Nvidia credit downgrade over governance and ‘key man’ risks](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/sp-considers-nvidia-credit-downgrade-over-governance-and-key-man-risks/)).

### Where scientists draw the line on AI agents

Ai2’s exploratory interviews with 17 US-based scientists identified five dimensions of trust in scientific AI agents: validation, privacy, objectivity, accountability, and transparency. Researchers emphasized that delegating work does not remove their responsibility to verify evidence, understand methods, protect sensitive data, and stand behind findings. Participants welcomed assistance with large datasets, coding, documentation, and repetitive administration, but wanted safeguards against unsupported claims, omitted contradictory evidence, unauthorized file access, and irreproducible analysis. Ai2 proposed scientist-informed evaluations that test research conduct alongside task performance, although these evaluations remain undeveloped proposals rather than established solutions. Researchers disagreed about appropriate uses of AI for generating ideas, writing papers, and preserving scientific expertise. Participants also highlighted tensions between safeguards that protect confidentiality and restrictions that could obstruct legitimate research. Ai2 advocates developing agents alongside scientific communities, with transparent action records, granular data controls, training, and clear institutional guidance. The small, nonrepresentative study identifies concerns and design priorities rather than establishing scientific consensus or measuring the performance of specific agents. ([Where scientists draw the line on AI agents](https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/where-scientists-draw-the-line-on-ai-agents)).

- [Google releases Cogentic papers on integer factorization, discrete logs and more](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/cogentic_research_papers): Google Research’s repository lists ten papers produced by Cogentic, dated October 30 through November 25, 2026. Six papers dated November 25 address integer factorization, discrete logarithms, approximate Nash equilibria, deterministic factoring and quadratic nonresidue construction, the log-rank conjecture, and graph edge reconstruction. Earlier papers address prophet inequalities for subadditive combinatorial auctions, fictitious-play convergence, sample compression for VC classes, and the intractability of approximate correlated equilibria in two-player convex games. The paper titles describe claimed results, including unconditional integer factorization in L_N 1/3, O 1 time and an exact polynomial-time algorithm for 1/5-approximate Nash equilibria in bimatrix games. The directory provides PDF links and generally releases its contents under the Apache 2.0 license, subject to exceptions in individual subdirectories. The project carries a disclaimer that it is not an official Google product.
- [Amazon and Meta reportedly in talks to lift blockade on AI agent Muse](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/amazon-and-meta-reportedly-in-talks-to-lift-blockade-on-ai-agent-muse/): Amazon and Meta are reportedly negotiating a deal that would let Meta’s AI shopping agent, Muse, connect directly to Amazon’s store, reversing a block that prevented Muse users from completing purchases two months earlier. Fortune reported that the companies have held multiple rounds of talks, with search-result presentation and advertising still under negotiation. Amazon’s concerns include potential damage to its advertising business and direct customer relationships, as well as purchase errors that could leave customers and sellers dissatisfied. Any agreement would reportedly require approval from both companies’ CEOs. Amazon and Meta had not responded to TechCrunch’s requests for comment.
- [Google’s patent for custom AI video feeds paints a bleak picture of entertainment’s future](https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1042221/google-my-channel-ai-video-patent): Google’s rejected patent for “My Channel” describes a system that would generate personalized AI videos and advertisements using demographic information, viewing history and device data, including IP addresses and GPS locations. The proposed system could incorporate news and social media activity, generate entire movies on Google’s servers and create digital avatars that cast users as lead characters using their photos, videos and audio. Google could appeal the rejection or submit an amended patent, but the proposal does not establish that a product will launch. Writer Charles Pulliam-Moore criticizes the concept as invasive and creatively impoverished, arguing that endlessly tailored entertainment could reinforce viewers’ existing tastes, discourage discovery and substitute AI imitations for intentionally crafted human storytelling.
- [Holy shit holy shit holy shit holy shit: The magnitude of what’s now happening](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10379): Scott Aaronson describes a wave of claimed AI-driven mathematical breakthroughs as a transformation in theoretical computer science that resembles the beginning of an AI singularity. In a November 25, 2026 post, Aaronson cites purported proofs from OpenAI and Anthropic addressing VP ≠ VNP and the Riemann Hypothesis. Aaronson reports that OpenAI claims to have established the matrix-multiplication exponent as exactly 9/4, with a lower bound matching an earlier upper bound. Aaronson also describes an OpenAI claim that the Fourier transform requires N log N ^ 1/2+o 1 linear gates, with matching upper and lower bounds. Aaronson regards the lower bounds as especially extraordinary because they resolve longstanding questions rather than merely improve existing algorithms. Aaronson argues that Lean proof certificates justify substantial confidence in the companies’ results, even if researchers need years to understand the proofs. Aaronson predicts that a proof of P ≠ NP may be approaching. Aaronson also says reliable sources have indicated that AI laboratories possess significant cryptographic breakthroughs, but Aaronson explicitly declines to endorse specific claims without details or direct confirmation. Aaronson balances excitement about mathematical discovery with anxiety about broader AI consequences and urges readers to spend time with family.
- [1Password Security Incident](https://1password.com/blog/update-on-nov-2026-security-incident): 1Password began investigating a security breach on November 19, 2026, after an attacker accessed an employee’s corporate GitHub account. The attacker copied private source code, accessed the company’s CI/CD system and pushed malicious code to backend services to download administrative records for a subset of users. The exposed records included names, email addresses, account and user identifiers, account timestamps, public keys, Secure Remote Password protocol parameters and other non-secret metadata. The records also included tokens from certain invitation, provisioning and recovery emails. 1Password said those tokens could not grant account access without an additional SSO sign-in or email verification code, and the company specifically notified the small number of users affected by provisioning or recovery tokens. 1Password said customer vault contents, vault-protection keys, account recovery keys, apps and browser extensions were unaffected. The company reported no internal evidence of account takeovers involving the exposed provisioning tokens and said the incident did not affect service availability or product security. 1Password disabled the compromised GitHub account, revoked the stolen token, removed attacker-created access and rotated or revoked potentially exposed internal credentials. The company also strengthened internal controls, engaged independent cybersecurity experts and expanded invitation and recovery verification codes from six numeric digits to ten alphanumeric characters. 1Password warned customers to watch for phishing messages involving invitations or account recovery and said it would never ask customers for their account password or Secret Key.
