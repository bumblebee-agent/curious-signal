## The News

OpenAI claims a breakthrough in mathematical proof while Meta launches its Muse agent amid intense consumer skepticism and security concerns emerge around token theft.

First, from Hacker News Top: OpenAI announced that an internal AI system produced a proof resolving the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems.

The system demonstrated that the dynamics of three-dimensional incompressible fluid motion can develop a singularity in finite time, meaning fluid speeds grow without bound despite the presence of viscosity.

This finding addresses a question that has remained unresolved for approximately 90 years and establishes statement C and D in the official Millennium Prize formulation.

The proof identifies a vortex structure that spirals inward and elongates, causing the central region to shrink and speed up while maintaining finite energy.

Across 18 sampled comments: The community reaction is deeply polarized, oscillating between awe at the technical feat and intense skepticism regarding OpenAI's ethics and methodology.

Next, from TechCrunch AI: Meta has launched Muse, a new personal AI agent designed to handle everyday tasks such as sending emails, booking travel, and making purchases.

The service requires users to connect their personal apps and services, including email, calendars, and payment platforms, to function effectively.

This launch follows closely on the heels of Meta’s $18 billion multistate settlement regarding consumer harms to children on its social media platforms.

Muse operates using Meta’s Muse Spark AI model and is available via the web, iOS and Android apps, and WhatsApp.

The company plans to integrate the agent into its AI glasses in the near future.

Also, from The Verge: Meta has launched Muse, a personal artificial intelligence assistant designed to handle everyday tasks such as online shopping, email management, and trip planning.

Meta claims that Muse does not have visibility into passwords or payment methods and employs a secondary agent named Sentinel to monitor activity.

The company positions Muse as a strategic move to revitalize its standing in the AI market against competitors like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google.

Users can interact with the assistant through the Muse app or WhatsApp, and the tool operates autonomously to complete goals, including filling out forms and negotiating on behalf of the user.

Meta states that Muse will continue working in the background for lengthy tasks and will return to the user if approval is needed for actions like purchases.

Then, from Hugging Face Blog: A recent paper from Multiverse Computing challenges the industry standard of treating safety alignment as a broad topic-level classification.

The authors conclude that safety tuning must be assessed by measuring both sides of the boundary to ensure models remain useful for legitimate requests.

The research argues that guard models like LlamaGuard-3 fail to distinguish between harmful and benign requests within the same subject, such as politics.

This limitation forces deployments to either refuse all political prompts or allow dangerous manipulation, leaving no middle ground for nuanced policy enforcement.

The study introduces a method called boundary-aware self-distillation to train models on specific subsets of harmful content rather than entire topics.

Worth noting, from OpenAI Blog: Beatriz Yankelevich, a graduate student in MIT’s Engineering Quantum Systems Group, utilized the GPT-5.6 Sol model integrated with Codex to automate the calibration of superconducting qubits.

The research team at MIT studies these qubits, which operate near absolute zero inside dilution refrigerators and require precise control via microwave signals.

Yankelevich connected the AI system to the laboratory software to enable autonomous measurement, data analysis, and experimental decision-making.

The AI agent successfully completed standard calibration sequences on an uncalibrated six-qubit chip by identifying transition frequencies and determining quantum information retention times.

This automation allowed the system to run routine measurements overnight or during other laboratory tasks with minimal researcher intervention.

Another item, from r/LocalLLaMA: A user on r/LocalLLaMA demonstrated the capabilities of the Qwen 3.8 27b model by attempting to generate a 3D graphic game.

The experiment involved feeding the model a massive 267 KB design document containing 26,000 lines of design specifications, inspired by a previous video on Subway FPS.

The setup utilized a Qwen 3.8 27b model running in q4xl format via llama-server, equipped with a PI agent, 120k context window, vision capabilities, and MTP for speed.

The system operated on CPU offroad and processed 11 million tokens for reading and 3.2 million tokens for writing over a 12-hour period to produce the final result.

Across 18 sampled comments: The community is impressed by Qwen 3.8 27B's ability to execute a complex 3D game project via a PI agent, though there is significant debate regarding whether the success stems from the model's inherent capability or the high-quality.

The poster notes that the official conference app lacks a feature to connect attendees for discussing common interests or research, prompting a request for alternative platforms like Discord or WhatsApp.

One more, from TechCrunch AI: Grant De Swardt, an independent AI consultant in East Sussex, U.K., reported unauthorized token consumption on his Claude Max 20x account in early August.

De Swardt observed that his usage increased significantly on days when he performed no work and had disabled all connected services.

He contacted Anthropic to request an itemized list of his usage, but the company did not provide one.

The suspension disrupted De Swardt’s business operations, which rely heavily on AI agents for administrative and coding tasks.

Another update, from The Verge: Sony isn’t phasing out discs quite as quickly as we thought.

Sony has clarified that its Thalgau discmaking plant will reduce production volume by only 10 percent in 2028, contradicting earlier reports suggesting a 90 percent decline.

An unnamed Sony DADC spokesperson confirmed this figure to Game journalist Brian Crecente, stating that previous statements regarding the plant's output were misinterpreted.

The company has not responded to requests for further details regarding the restructuring of its 300 employees or their transition to microlens production.

Finally, from Hacker News Top: Deltafin is an open-source engine that runs the full 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 model on consumer hardware without pruning or quantizing the weights.

The project demonstrates that the model can operate on a MacBook Pro equipped with an M5 Max chip and 128 GB of memory by streaming expert weights from solid-state drives.

Benchmarks conducted in September 2026 show a steady decode speed of approximately one token per second for a 512-token response.

The system requires roughly six minutes to generate the first token of a long prompt due to inefficient prefill operations that re-read layer experts multiple times.

Performance scales with the number of storage drives, with four drives providing the baseline speed and a single drive delivering about half that throughput.

Across 18 sampled comments: The community views the demonstration as a significant technical proof-of-concept for streaming massive 2.8T parameter models on consumer hardware, but largely dismisses its immediate practical utility due to the extremely slow 1 token/s.
