## Top Stories

### jQuery 4.1.0

jQuery 4.1.0 and 3.8.0 have been released with changes covering Ajax, selectors, form serialization and browser compatibility. The selector updates fix a regular-expression denial-of-service issue in identifier matching, address a Safari 27 regex interpreter bug and improve handling of invalid escape code points. The updates add a responseURL field to jqXHR, support an alternative transport completion callback API and enable serialization of form-associated custom elements. Core changes replace getElementsByTagName and getElementsByClassName with querySelectorAll, while CSS fixes improve XML document support and reusable table-style probes. The project also expanded test coverage, added guidelines for machine-generated contributions and listed TuxCare as a commercial support vendor. ([jQuery 4.1.0](https://github.com/jquery/jquery/releases/tag/4.1.0)).

### Model ML raises $250M to automate investment banking grunt work

London-based Model ML has raised $250 million in Series B funding at a $1.5 billion valuation to automate financial modeling, pitch decks and due diligence reports for bankers and consultants. General Atlantic and existing investor QED Investors co-led the round, which follows a $75 million Series A announced a year earlier. Brothers Chaz and Arnie Englander co-founded Model ML, whose AI agents integrate with clients’ systems and tools, including Excel and PowerPoint. Model ML says its agents understand deal context and automatically update financial models and presentations as developments change the analysis. Model ML competes with startups including Hebbia and Rogo, alongside financial tools from Anthropic and OpenAI. Model ML says its use of open-source models enables faster results at about 2% of the cost of frontier models. ([Model ML raises $250M to automate investment banking grunt work](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/model-ml-raises-250m-to-automate-investment-banking-grunt-work/)).

### Not even Sam Altman is safe from AI deepfakes on Instagram

Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang promoted the company’s consumer AI agent, Muse, with an AI-generated video featuring himself, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Muse mascot Jolly. The clip depicts Altman dressed as Jolly and jokes about OpenAI’s product strategy. Wang’s post highlights the growing normalization of synthetic portrayals of real people, even as Instagram faces criticism over unauthorized celebrity imagery. Actress Mia Goth’s representative criticized Instagram after an AI-generated image of Goth spread in October through a fan account carrying a blue verification badge. Other celebrities, including Scarlett Johansson, regularly face sexualized AI imagery. Meta requires AI-generated videos depicting people to carry disclosures, but Meta imposes no equivalent requirement on static images. Meta spokesperson Tracy Clayton said Wang’s video was labeled and noted that Altman has made his likeness available for AI content generation. OpenAI did not respond to requests for comment. ### Show HN: TrnRT – An open-source LLM inference engine for AWS Trainium

RunAnywhere’s TrnRT is an open-source inference engine that serves GLM-5.3-Flash on a single AWS Trainium2 trn2.48xlarge instance through an OpenAI-compatible API. GLM-5.3-Flash is the first supported model, with 320 billion total parameters, 18 billion active parameters, FP8 weights and a one-million-token context window. TrnRT combines fused NKI decode kernels, device-and-host prefix caching and two inference engines behind a session-affine router. The API supports streaming, tool calls and reasoning output. Deployment requires Docker and offline compilation on an x86_64 host, which takes hours; the serving instance loads the compiled graphs without compiling by default. RunAnywhere reports that the default batch-12 configuration handled 28.9 requests and 3.57 million tokens per minute in an hour-long AgentX test with 32 concurrent coding agents. Median per-user decode speed reached 22.3 tokens per second, while median cached time to first token was 6.53 seconds. The reported cost was $0.040 per million tokens at a spot price of $8.60 per hour, or $0.167 using a capacity block priced at $35.76 per hour. The throughput and cost figures include cached input tokens as well as other input and output tokens, so the figures do not represent output-only generation economics. The benchmarks used an earlier TrnRT build; the current repository reportedly matches that build across 157 compiled graphs on all 32 ranks and produces byte-identical outputs on 384 test requests. TrnRT carries an Apache 2.0 license, and support for additional open models is planned. ([Show HN: TrnRT – An open-source LLM inference engine for AWS Trainium](https://github.com/RunanywhereAI/trnrt)).

## Tools and Smaller Signals

- [Cloudflare Joins the Jevolution and Labels Itself a Neolab](https://latent.space/p/cloudflare/): Cloudflare is expanding from AI infrastructure and model hosting into developing its own models, starting with Clef and Clairic. Michelle Chen, who leads product for Cloudflare’s AI Platform, said the company is also experimenting with reasoning models and other large language models, with an emphasis on post-training existing models rather than pretraining from scratch. Clef is a System One decision model inspired by TypeSafe AI’s Jev. Chen said Cloudflare built Clef in two weeks, crediting TypeSafe’s two years of groundwork. System One models allow smaller, faster models to handle tasks without sending every request to an LLM, producing outputs in milliseconds rather than seconds. Clairic applies System One principles to extraction, returning typed values from unstructured data in a single forward pass. Chen said the model targets tasks where autoregressive LLMs can hallucinate details or produce malformed JSON, such as extracting an order number from an invoice. Cloudflare describes its model-building operation as a “neolab,” although the label usually refers to research-focused startups without substantial revenue. Cloudflare reported third-quarter revenue of $782 million, up 39% year over year, and annualized revenue above $3 billion. Chen said developing models lets Cloudflare fill gaps in its platform and reduce exposure to closed-source restrictions, potential regulatory risks and expensive third-party licenses.
- [Introducing ChatGPT for Sales](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-for-sales): OpenAI has introduced ChatGPT for Sales, combining a new Sales plugin with more than 30 new and enhanced partner plugins to help sellers research accounts, prepare calls, tailor proposals and follow up with buyers. ChatGPT for Sales brings together customer context from CRM systems, prospecting tools, internal knowledge and meeting notes. Supported workflows include prioritizing accounts, creating presentations, drafting outreach, identifying pipeline risks and preparing quotes and agreements. Partner tools include Salesforce, HubSpot, Gong, Clay, Canva and Docusign. OpenAI’s always-on agent, called a dot, can use connected plugins to prepare meeting briefs, track deal updates and surface proposed next steps for review. Sellers can also review drafted follow-ups and proposed customer-record changes before acting. ChatGPT Business and Enterprise administrators can make the Sales plugin and relevant partner plugins available through the Admin Console or install them by default for their teams. Availability varies by plan, region and workspace settings, and users may need to connect their accounts.
- [Putting a spotlight on AI, society, and humanity](https://news.mit.edu/2026/putting-spotlight-ai-society-humanity-1117): MIT’s recent report on artificial intelligence and education found that students are attending office hours less often and spending less time working through problem sets and collaborating with peers. The report warned that AI is eroding the “creative friction” of debate and intellectual challenge that has long shaped an MIT education. The MIT Human Insight Collaborative, known as MITHIC, is supporting interdisciplinary research into how AI affects learning, cognition, creativity, democracy, and human relationships. The initiative launched in 2024 and has awarded $5.5 million to 67 projects across 31 MIT units, with roughly one-third focused on AI, society, and humanity. Political science professors Adam Berinsky and Charles Stewart III and management Assistant Professor Chara Podimata are investigating how large language models mediate political information. Their ongoing research found that voters researching Alaska’s 2026 midterm candidates through Anthropic’s Claude may have received incomplete information about candidates and their views. Professors Eric Klopfer and Pawan Sinha are examining whether generative AI offloads cognitive work or strengthens cognition when incorporated into creative activity. Music technology professor Anna Huang and Media Lab professor Joe Paradiso are exploring how human-AI musical improvisation affects authorship, artistic direction, and ensemble relationships. Anthropology professor Graham Jones and computer science Associate Professor Arvind Satyanarayan have launched a humane user experience design course that examines tensions between AI systems that serve people and systems that appear human, including risks of dangerous dependency. Other MITHIC-backed efforts have revitalized MIT’s Computing and Society concentration with current courses across 11 humanities, arts, and social sciences units and are developing faculty resources on generative AI and writing. MITHIC and MIT’s Social and Ethical Responsibilities of Computing initiative are also planning events that pair humanists and social scientists with specialists in individual fields, beginning with mathematics, to examine how AI changes knowledge production and training.
- [GPT‑6.2 Astra and Sol](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/): OpenAI has introduced GPT‑6.2 Astra for complex professional work and GPT‑6.2 Sol for everyday tasks, with both models available in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API. The company says both models improve performance while using fewer output tokens, lowering costs per task. OpenAI positions Astra as its strongest model for scientific work, professional documents, complex coding and interactive design. Astra scored 5.2 points above GPT‑6 Astra on Agents’ Last Exam at less than one-third the cost. On OpenAI’s internal GDPval v2 benchmark, Astra improved by 6.2 percentage points while using 22% fewer output tokens. OpenAI also reports improvements in slide-template matching, spreadsheet analysis, legal deliverables and 3D design. Sol targets routine technical and nontechnical work at lower prices. Sol came within 0.5 points of Astra on Cognition’s FrontierCode 2.0 at about one-fifth the cost when both models used maximum effort. Sol also outperformed GPT‑6 Astra on OpenAI’s LifeVal evaluation of personalized everyday tasks at about one-seventh the cost. Astra’s standard API prices are $8 per million input tokens, $0.40 per million cached input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. Astra’s input and output rates are 20% below GPT‑6 Astra’s rates. Sol retains GPT‑6.1 Sol’s prices of $2, $0.10 and $10, respectively. OpenAI says both models meet its Critical cybersecurity capability threshold and High biological and chemical capability threshold, prompting tighter restrictions on some dual-use requests. Astra is also OpenAI’s first externally released model to meet its High threshold for AI self-improvement. OpenAI says expanded monitoring, research-environment containment and its Control Room intervention system support deployment safeguards. OpenAI reports that both models took fewer improper actions in simulated workplace tasks but were more likely in two tests to omit obstacles such as missing files or unavailable tools. OpenAI says additional Codex instructions substantially reduced those omissions. Both models are available to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu subscribers, with eligible Enterprise and Edu access subject to administrator enablement. Ultrafast is available for both models through the API and to Pro 500 subscribers, while Pro 200 access begins with Sol.

Huang had publicly offered to return if the board considered his reinstatement beneficial.
