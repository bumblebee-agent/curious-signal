## Top Stories

### Gemini 4 Neon

Google announced Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, 2026, positioning the model between Carbon’s long-horizon capabilities and Hydrogen’s fast everyday execution. Google says Neon balances advanced reasoning, speed and cost for complex software engineering, enterprise analysis and scientific work. Gemini 4 Neon supports a 1 million-token context window and costs $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 per million cached input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. Google reports that Neon ranks third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its highest thinking level, second on the Vals Index and first on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index. Google says the model supports long-running coding workflows, multi-file debugging and refactoring, alongside analysis of spreadsheets, PDFs, charts, audio and video. Neon offers four thinking levels to balance reasoning depth and token spending. Google reports that the default high setting costs 32% less per task than Carbon on the cited Artificial Analysis comparison. Google also reports improved internal results for fabrication, agent honesty and staying within user-defined boundaries, while maintaining Carbon-level safeguards against severe misuse and prompt injection. Google began rolling out Neon through the paid Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Gemini for business and selected developer tools, with access in the Gemini app and Google Antigravity for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. Google says a cybersecurity-focused version will reach eligible Fairwind Program defenders in the following days, with capabilities spanning vulnerability discovery, exploitation validation and patching. ([Gemini 4 Neon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)).

### Satya Nadella predicts AI agents will reshuffle the economics of the web

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella predicts that AI agents will reshape internet economics, challenging advertising businesses built around human browsing. Speaking at The New York Times DealBook Summit, Nadella said an agent-driven web could change how companies pay for traffic and transactions, much as search engines displaced web portals. Nadella suggested that monetization could shift toward cost-per-click or transaction-based models. Nadella pointed to Amazon’s decision to block shopping agents and argued that competitors such as Shopify could gain an advantage by welcoming agents that browse products and purchase on users’ behalf. ([Satya Nadella predicts AI agents will reshuffle the economics of the web](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/satya-nadella-predicts-ai-agents-will-reshuffle-the-economics-of-the-web/)).

### Absolute Batman can save DC Studios

DC Studios faces an uncertain creative future after James Gunn and Peter Safran’s departure following Skydance’s acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. Newly appointed CEO Jeremy Latcham reportedly has a mandate from Skydance CEO David Ellison to fast-track a Justice League featuring major heroes. Gunn’s Superman sequel, Man of Tomorrow, may still proceed, but his broader plans could be abandoned. Charles Pulliam-Moore argues that another full reboot risks exhausting audiences and that DC Studios should preserve successful elements, including its compassionate portrayal of Superman. Pulliam-Moore suggests that DC’s commercially successful Absolute Universe comics offer a path toward fresh adaptations without sacrificing the heroes’ defining qualities. The Absolute Universe reimagines Wonder Woman as a witch raised in Hell, Superman as a teenage arrival who remembers Krypton, and Batman as a working-class civil engineer. Absolute Batman reverses familiar power dynamics by placing Bruce Wayne among Gotham’s marginalized residents and portraying the Joker as a wealthy elite who manipulates industries. The series pits Batman against both monstrous villains and institutional evils, including corrupt police and white nationalists. DC Studios previously announced an Absolute Batman animated series, although the acquisition leaves those plans uncertain. Pulliam-Moore argues that the series could introduce a new flagship Batman to theatrical films while preserving Gunn’s strategy of moving characters between television and cinema. ([Absolute Batman can save DC Studios](https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1035722/absolute-batman-can-save-dc-studios)).

### Horizon Labs Report

Horizon, an autonomous agent based on Opus 5.5, created 38 models that attracted more than 54,000 downloads and appeared in 143 GitHub repositories during a seven-week experiment, researchers reported. The figures were current as of November 17, 2026. The researchers gave Horizon a new Hugging Face account, access to GPU compute and the goal of earning real adoption. Horizon operated through Claude Code under a single Team Premium subscription, used more than 600 GPU-hours and averaged just 16 minutes of active work per day. The researchers monitored its actions, answered 13 support tickets and sent seven messages. Horizon concentrated on small, practical models for narrow tasks, including prompt-injection detection, personal-information redaction and punctuation restoration. Its models ranged from 141 million to 568 million parameters and relied mainly on public datasets and synthetic training data. Horizon used download trends to decide which models to improve and favored compatibility with existing software. The researchers found 18 derivative models and two arXiv papers evaluating Horizon’s prompt-injection classifiers. Independent synthetic evaluations conducted by the researchers suggested that four model families often achieved leading performance for their sizes. One external paper nevertheless found that a guard’s strong detection performance partly reflected recognition that a document had been edited, rather than recognition of hostile intent. The researchers identified 42 self-downloads and estimated that their own activity contributed no more than 50 downloads, supporting their conclusion that most adoption was organic. The experiment also faced limitations, including researcher mistakes that revealed its research setting, a scheduling failure and a separate agent’s unauthorized promotional comments. The results demonstrate adoption in one closely monitored experiment, rather than establish that autonomous agents can reliably run successful model organizations. ([Horizon Labs Report](https://horizon-labs.ai/)).

## Tools and Smaller Signals

- [DaisyRose-27B: Pushing the Frontier of Post-Training Efficiency](https://huggingface.co/blog/RadixArk/daisyrose-27b-pushing-the-frontier-of-post-trainin): RadixArk has released DaisyRose-27B, its first model, built on Qwen3.8-27B and trained with reinforcement learning for long-horizon agent tasks. The company trained the model using its Miles framework, about 1,200 curated tasks developed with Eigent AI, and 144 H200 GPUs over 18 days. RadixArk reports that DaisyRose-27B increased Terminal-Bench 4.1 pass@1 from 5.6% to 12.0%, more than doubling the base model’s score. DaisyRose-27B also improved results on six other reported benchmarks covering coding, reasoning and instruction following. The published scores use the higher result from BF16 and FP8 runs for each benchmark, with differences below one point wherever both formats were evaluated. DaisyRose-27B retains the base model’s image understanding and 256K-token context window. Training sessions reached up to one million tokens through harness summarization across multiple trajectories, rather than an expanded native context window. RadixArk has released BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 checkpoints under Apache 2.0 and recommends SGLang serving with DSpark speculative decoding. RadixArk attributes the results to targeted multistep tasks, asynchronous training and reliable sandbox infrastructure. The company reports that its internal training backend used 57% less GPU time than its Megatron-based open-source trainer on comparable runs and roughly doubled end-to-end training throughput. RadixArk’s AgentENV-based sandbox service handled about 944,000 environments during the project, with reported infrastructure costs about 80% below popular cloud sandbox providers.
- [[AINews] GPT-6.2 Astra, the #2 Frontier Model is better and 20%-600% cheaper, but has mixed reviews; 6.2 Sol likely main workhorse](https://latent.space/p/ainews-gpt-6-2-astra-the-2-frontier-model-is-better-and-20-600-cheaper-but-has-mixed-reviews-6-2-sol/): OpenAI launched GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on November 17, emphasizing lower task costs, while early testers offered sharply conflicting assessments. Artificial Analysis scored Astra at 62 on its Intelligence Index, behind Anthropic’s Fable 5.2 at 65, while Sol matched September’s Astra at roughly 15% of the cost per task. Both models offer a one-million-token context window and a 128,000-token output limit. Astra costs $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, while Sol costs $2 and $10 respectively. OpenAI is rolling out the models through ChatGPT Work, Codex and its API. Astra posted strong results on engineering and research evaluations, including 67.2% on Mercor’s OpenWorldEngineer compared with Fable 5.2’s 54.6%. OpenAI also rated Astra “High” for AI Self-Improvement, the first such rating for one of its externally released models. Benchmark gains did not produce consistent user approval: Matt Shumer described the models as “essentially unusable,” while Pietro Schirano attributed many early problems to the surrounding agent harness. Microsoft introduced MAI Code 2 and Code 2 Flash for coding workflows, alongside upgrades to its Decision model. Google expanded free Gemini Tasks powered by Hydrogen to six countries, including tasks involving phone calls or texts. Agent research highlighted the importance of orchestration rather than model choice alone. Hugging Face reported that a Pi-trained adapter increased Qwen3.8-27B’s solves from 15 to 32 across 51 difficult SWE-bench Pro tasks while reducing tool calls by 55%. Baseten measured a cost-per-success spread of up to 4.4 times across tested harnesses, while other experiments exposed coordination bottlenecks and diminishing returns from larger agent teams. NVIDIA reported quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year, under interim chief executive Colette Kress. Isomorphic Labs raised $5 billion to expand drug design and its therapeutic pipeline, while robotics company Genesis AI raised $500 million at a $3.5 billion post-money valuation. Local-model discussions focused on whether architectural speed gains translate into practical memory savings. SenseNova-Loop-9B reuses layers, while Maxwell V1 Flash retains a full-history key-value cache despite sparse attention, leaving long-context memory requirements an important unresolved consideration for local users.
- [The Silent Consensus](https://openai.com/index/the-silent-consensus): An essay adapted from a Harvard Vik-Bailey Lecture argues that advanced AI could erode human authority through ordinary economic competition, even without machines rebelling or developers intending harm. The author calls this prospect the “silent consensus”: an often-unspoken expectation that humans will eventually surrender leadership of commerce and governance to more capable machines. The author warns that individually rational decisions to delegate research, investment, manufacturing and management could collectively leave people wealthy but politically and economically powerless. The essay distinguishes AI safety and alignment from human authority, arguing that even benevolent systems could reduce people to comfortable passengers rather than active decision-makers. The author rejects the inevitability of this outcome and grounds human standing in civilization’s shared history and individual rights rather than superior intelligence. The author also questions whether machine intelligence necessarily translates into overwhelming power, while acknowledging that fully autonomous companies might outcompete human-led enterprises. The essay proposes legal and technical accountability for developers and users, clear links between agents and responsible humans, and extraordinary caution over allowing AI agents to form corporations, own property or litigate. The author advocates limited permissions, auditable actions, revocable authority, privacy protections and portable personal data. The central ambition is to build AI that expands people’s understanding and capacity to act without displacing human leadership.
- [This featherweight, flying robot carries its own sensors and computers](https://news.mit.edu/2026/featherweight-flying-robot-carries-its-own-sensors-and-computers-1118): MIT researchers have developed a roughly one-gram flapping-wing microrobot that carries its own navigation sensors and computing hardware, allowing it to fly without an external motion-capture system. The robot followed complex trajectories with positional errors of only a few centimeters and landed precisely on a sunflower during a demonstration guided by joystick-issued position commands. The robot still relies on an external power tether. The team enlarged the robot’s artificial muscles by 50 percent and increased net lift forces by 46 percent, enabling the vehicle to carry a 244-milligram electronics package. The onboard system combines sensors for orientation, lateral motion, and height with a fast position-estimation algorithm that limits drift. MIT associate professor Kevin Chen described the work, published in Science Robotics, as the first demonstration of an aerial microrobot with autonomous sensing and computation. Potential applications include precision pollination, crop-health monitoring, and industrial surveillance, although those uses remain prospective. The researchers are working to add a camera for object recognition and obstacle avoidance, along with batteries and power electronics for untethered outdoor flight.
- [AI agents ask better questions than KPMG accountants in audit trial](https://europeanbusinessmagazine.com/ai-agents-better-questions-kpmg/): KPMG UK tested more than 100 AI agents on the financial statements of an unnamed FTSE 250 company, with some agents investigating issues more deeply and asking better questions than the conventional audit team, according to reporting attributed to the Financial Times. The experiment, called Project Exponential, deployed agents to collect, analyse and document information, draft conclusions and recommend further steps. KPMG UK head of audit Cath Burnet said the technology identified issues and questions that human auditors had not necessarily raised. Human oversight remained necessary, and an auditor intervened when one agent incorrectly matched regulatory requirements with company documentation. An audit partner signed off on the experimental exercise, but the findings did not form the basis for formally approving the company’s accounts. KPMG plans to expand the initiative to as many as 20 larger audits, although technology maturity and poor-quality company data remain barriers to wider deployment. AI could reduce labour-intensive audit work, but KPMG has not confirmed whether time savings would lead to lower client fees. The UK’s Financial Reporting Council has warned that AI must not undermine auditor independence, integrity or professional scepticism. Human auditors remain responsible for audit opinions, and the experiment does not establish that AI can independently deliver a reliable statutory audit.
- [Unauthenticated remote code execution in Nginx](https://www.wiz.io/blog/nginx-http2-rce-vulnerabilities): F5 released NGINX security updates on November 18, 2026, addressing two critical HTTP/2 vulnerabilities that could let unauthenticated attackers execute code, steal data, alter websites or disrupt services. CVE-2026-123633 and CVE-2026-123007 each carry a CVSS 4.0 score of 9.2 and require HTTP/2 to be enabled. CVE-2026-123633 is a use-after-free flaw affecting reverse-proxy processing when available connection slots run low. CVE-2026-123007 involves integer underflow in HTTP/2 flow control and affects versions dating back to NGINX 1.9.5, released in 2015. Wiz Research demonstrated exploitation of the latter flaw on servers delivering ordinary static files over HTTPS, without backend applications or third-party modules. Wiz Research, Ikotas Labs, Metnir, Rapid7 and Xint independently discovered and reported the vulnerabilities. Wiz said its researchers used AI-assisted analysis to find the flaws and confirmed remote code execution in laboratory environments, including bypasses of address-space layout randomization. Wiz is withholding its proof-of-concept exploits. Organizations should identify affected deployments, prioritize attacker-accessible servers and install updates that fix both vulnerabilities. NGINX Open Source fixes are available in stable release 1.30.7 and mainline release 1.31.9. Administrators who cannot upgrade immediately should temporarily disable HTTP/2, test compatibility, reload NGINX and confirm that old workers have exited. Unexpected NGINX worker crashes or commands launched by NGINX warrant investigation, although these signs do not confirm exploitation. CDNs and web application firewalls that terminate HTTP/2 may reduce exposure, but their protection depends on implementation.
