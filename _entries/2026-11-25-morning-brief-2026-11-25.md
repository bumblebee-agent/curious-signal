## The News

The main reports are dated November 24, 2026.

Main themes over the latest cycle: agents, 2.5, 27b.

First, from Hacker News: Songe is a local learning layer that uses corrections, preferences, retries and tool errors from AI-agent conversations to personalize a model. Songe sits between an OpenAI-compatible agent and a local model server, records interactions and learns during idle periods. The project says all processing stays on the machine, with no account, telemetry or cloud service. Songe evaluates the trained version against the current version and adopts it only if checks show clear improvement without degrading successful behavior. Users can remove preferences or roll back learned versions.

Next, from Hacker News: PrismML has introduced Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, a Qwen3.8 27B-based model that retains its predecessor’s 5.9 GB footprint while targeting stronger local coding agents and multi-step tool use. PrismML says the update reduces excessive thinking and repetitive loops, completing tasks in fewer turns and with fewer tokens. PrismML’s reported evaluations show SWE-bench Verified scores rising from 60.8 to 75.6 and AppWorld scores increasing from 51.2 to 90.5 compared with Bonsai 2 27B. The newer model narrows the gap with the full-precision base model on several benchmarks, although its LiveCodeBench v6 score slips from 83.8 to 83.0. PrismML reports generation speeds of 291 tokens per second on an NVIDIA H100 and 204 tokens per second on an RTX 4090 with multi-token prediction.

Also, from TechCrunch AI: ShellBench, a new benchmark for everyday AI agent work, found that even the strongest models tested from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta failed more than a third of attempts. Across all tested models, 15.7% of attempts violated at least one safety requirement, while roughly 10% of otherwise correct attempts also failed a safety criterion. Researchers developed ShellBench using more than 10 million anonymized, real-world agent interactions to evaluate multi-step personal tasks involving messy emails, calendars, spreadsheets, receipts, and other records. OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra achieved the highest score, but the model completed all three repetitions of a task successfully less than half the time.

Then, from Hugging Face Blog: Microduck can learn local obstacle avoidance without retraining its walking policy through a workflow using NVIDIA Isaac Lab and Newton simulation. A separate navigation controller adjusts gamepad-requested forward, lateral and turning velocities at 50 Hz, while the frozen walking policy handles balance and joint actions. The controller uses an 8×8 time-of-flight sensor that measures depth at 15 Hz. Simulated training adds noise, delays and missing readings, while randomized courses combine cones with SimReady boxes, bins and cabinets. Rewards favor safe progress toward goals and penalize collisions, falls, abrupt commands and risky approaches.

Worth noting, from The Verge: A federal jury in New York found Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 criminal counts in a case involving the theft of US trade secrets, according to NBC News and Bloomberg. The years-long case accused Huawei of stealing source code and proprietary technology from companies including T-Mobile, Cisco and Motorola. The convictions included money laundering, bank fraud, wire fraud and conspiracy to defraud the US. The jury acquitted Huawei of criminal racketeering conspiracy and obstruction of justice. Huawei plans to appeal and could face fines, forfeiture and restitution.

Another item, from MIT News AI: TangiMap uses AI to repurpose physical knobs, faders and buttons for software tasks, bringing tactile controls to photo editing, music performance, 3D modeling and animation. MIT doctoral student and CSAIL researcher Yunyi Zhu developed the system with Boston University assistant professor Chang Xiao. TangiMap gathers context about an application, a user’s habits and an input device through software interfaces or screenshot analysis, then uses a large language model to map digital functions to physical controls. The researchers demonstrated mixers controlling color adjustments in Adobe Lightroom and model properties in Blender, alongside a control pad triggering expressions and movements in Adobe Character Animator. Physical controls could support eyes-free operation and simultaneous adjustments, but improvements to user experience remain unverified.

Finally, from OpenAI Blog: iFood says its Remote Agents Platform has completed more than 25,000 engineering tasks across an ecosystem of more than 15,000 code repositories, consuming over 27 billion tokens at an average cost of US$0.04 per run. The company reports that its internal security platform more than doubled both fix accuracy and the number of vulnerabilities resolved after adopting the system. The platform lets teams submit prompts and environment requirements through a REST API, delegates execution to isolated remote sandboxes, and returns results asynchronously, including merge requests and generated artifacts. Deterministic steps handle repository cloning, branch setup and publication, while GPT‑5.6 Luna performs the agentic coding work.
