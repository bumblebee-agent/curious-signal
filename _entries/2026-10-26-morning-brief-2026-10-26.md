Also, from Hacker News Top: Eugene, Oregon, police estimated that fulfilling a public records request about officer Justin Peckels would require at least 44,816 staff hours and cost $2,484,599.04. Local news outlet Double Sided Media sought records concerning potential misconduct between 2018 and 2026, including complaints involving Peckels punching a resident during a traffic stop. A separate request concerning former officer Eric Klinko received a $195,869.52 estimate, bringing the combined cost to nearly $2.7 million. The city said it could not narrow the potential record-holders and would need to search records belonging to all current and former city employees. The department required payment before continuing processing and warned that additional costs could follow.

Across 18 sampled comments: The thread is predominantly frustrated, with commenters viewing prohibitive records fees and prolonged delays as ways to undermine public-access laws. Comparisons with other jurisdictions raise questions about oversight and meaningful enforcement.

Then, from Hacker News Top: The Digital Dissident Devroom at FOSDEM 2027 is inviting proposals on free software that protects people facing censorship, surveillance and repression. The programme will focus on the complete chain of tools that at-risk users need, from accessing blocked networks and communicating privately to gathering evidence and publishing without exposing sources. Organisers want technical lessons and cross-project collaboration rather than product pitches. Topics include censorship circumvention, metadata-resistant messaging, secure reporting, device security, usability under threat and censorship measurement.

Across 18 sampled comments: The discussion supports resisting censorship but questions whether software alone can overcome infrastructure control and dominant platforms’ influence over discovery and link sharing.

Worth noting, from The Verge: iFixit gave Apple’s iPhone Duo a 5 out of 10 repairability score, the highest score it has awarded a foldable. The teardown found that the internal display’s matte nano-texture cover scatters reflections to help conceal the crease, while angled oval subpixels may further reduce visible artifacts. The nano-texture outer layer is relatively easy to replace independently of the OLED panel, but repairs to the display itself remain expensive and difficult. Dust entered the ports, speaker grilles and hinge, although less test dust reached the phone’s interior than in the Samsung Z Fold8 or Pixel 11 Pro Fold. iFixit suspects that a hinge component may sometimes fail to descend properly, potentially concentrating pressure near an accordion cable and the battery edge.

Another item, from r/LocalLLaMA: CrowdGPT’s developer is seeking feedback on barriers to contributing to a collaborative 1-billion-parameter LLM proof of concept. The post describes training on participants’ PCs, merging contributions into a global model, public training data and weights, and community input on project direction. It asks whether volunteers would donate GPU time or want incentives such as inference credits; no incentive decision or resolution is supplied.

Across 14 sampled comments: Reaction is mixed: commenters welcome community-led LLM training, but repeatedly question how untrusted contributions can be checked for poisoning or backdoors.

Also in the mix, from The Verge: Scott Walker’s 2006 album The Drift offers a genuinely unsettling alternative to playful Halloween music, exploring death, disease and historical tragedy through references to 9/11, the Srebrenica genocide and the hanging of Benito Mussolini and Clara Petacci. The experimental arrangements combine dissonant strings, seasick guitars, animal sounds, mechanical noise and abrupt silences with Walker’s distinctive vocals. The record can be challenging to finish in one sitting, but its disturbing atmosphere rewards sustained listening. The Drift is available on Bandcamp and major streaming services.

Shifting gears, from TechCrunch AI: Grok has released a seven-minute opening episode of “The Odyssey,” three months after Elon Musk promised a full-length, “historically accurate” adaptation. The project was created with Wonder Studios and director Dev Walker. Musk previously criticized director Christopher Nolan’s adaptation for casting nonwhite and transgender actors. Nolan’s film, released in July, emphasized practical filmmaking and became a major box-office success. TechCrunch weekend editor Anthony Ha found some of Grok’s imagery convincingly realistic but criticized the episode’s prolonged narration, stilted dialogue and lack of artistic vision or emotional engagement. Ha argued that the seven-minute release offered little reason to watch further installments.

One more, from r/LocalLLaMA: AtomicChat released GGUF quantizations of Nanbeige4.3-33B on October 25, the model’s release day. Nanbeige’s Apache-2.0 model has 33 billion parameters, a dense Llama architecture, a 512K context window and thinking enabled by default. AtomicChat’s measurements indicate substantial sensitivity to quantization. The plain Q80 file occupies 35.2 GB and matches the BF16 original’s next-token choice 98.0% of the time, while plain Q4KM occupies 20.1 GB and achieves 89.9% agreement. AtomicChat’s Atomic Dynamic variants use an importance matrix and selectively retain higher precision in parts of the model. The 20.3 GB AD-IQ4XS variant achieves 92.0% next-token agreement and roughly one-third less KL divergence than plain Q4KM at nearly the same disk size.

Another update, from TechCrunch AI: Apollo Global Management chief economist Torsten Slok says corporate financial data show no evidence of an AI payoff beyond the largest tech companies. Slok examined the S&P 500 excluding Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla, finding that profit margins and revenue per worker appear flat despite substantial AI investment. Slok argues that cheaper intelligence may not translate into stronger revenue growth or higher margins because business judgment depends on accountability, trust, culture and ethical trade-offs. Recent studies have also found that AI has yet to deliver significant productivity gains.

Looking ahead, from TechCrunch AI: AI-powered pendants, rings, wristbands, and handheld devices are competing to become everyday productivity tools and personal companions, with products ranging from conversation recorders to emotional-support assistants. Amazon’s Bee lineup records conversations and generates reminders, notes, and daily takeaways. Amazon acquired Bee in July 2025 and introduced the $74.99 Bee Frontier and $99.99 Bee Eclipse in October 2026, with optional Alexa connectivity. Bee’s companion app is currently iOS-only and requires a $19 monthly subscription. Conversation-focused devices include the $99 Limitless pendant, the $89 Omi, and Plaud’s $159 NotePin.
