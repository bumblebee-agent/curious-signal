## The News

The main reports are dated November 21, 2026.

AI-assisted mathematical verification of the Riemann Hypothesis dominates headlines alongside growing tensions over AI surveillance and autonomous weapons regulation.

First, from Hacker News: LookAtMyQuant is a quantization compiler that selects codes for model parameters to minimize output damage while adhering to a specific size budget. The tool achieves a 12× lower Kullback-Leibler KL divergence than Q4_K_M on the Gemma 4 E2B model at equivalent file sizes. Shin Megami Boson developed LookAtMyQuant 2, which compresses Qwen3.5-4B to 1.23 GB and solves 330 of 540 exact-answer math problems, outperforming Prism ML’s Bonsai 2 Ternary 4B which solves 328. The recipe applies post-training quantization using trellis codes, Fisher information-based bit allocation, and distillation on digit-weighted math solutions.

Next, from Hacker News: PcapAnalyzer is a free, open-source beta tool that identifies network faults within seconds by analyzing uploaded.pcap or.pcapng captures locally on the user’s machine. The software employs 27 detectors to examine TCP connectivity issues, path anomalies, name and address services, application responses, tunnel statuses, and security threats such as port scans and cleartext logins. It also assesses capture quality by flagging limitations like truncated packets or one-way traffic that might skew results. The interface provides Wireshark-style views including packet lists, expert info, and flow diagrams, while an optional AI investigation feature allows users to ask follow-up questions using their own Anthropic API keys.

Also, from Hacker News: On November 20, 2026, Alpöge released a Lean library consisting of 17 million lines of code that formalizes this proof. A joint statement from these groups, endorsed by the Lean Free Software Foundation and the Mathlib Initiative, confirms the correctness of the work. The verification process relied on the fact that the codebase depends directly on Mathlib, a well-established mathematical library. Community members compiled the code and confirmed that it builds without errors or failures.

Then, from TechCrunch AI: A growing number of artificial intelligence agents function as textable personal assistants that remember context and complete tasks on behalf of users. While Instinct leads the market with a $10 billion valuation following a $1 billion Series C funding round, numerous other competitors offer specialized features for families, travel, and professional work. Ask Proxy differentiates itself by aiming to anticipate user needs and includes a work-focused version that keeps personal data off company systems.

Worth noting, from TechCrunch AI: Signal announced on Friday that it is restricting users' ability to take screenshots within its secure messaging app. President Meredith Whittaker cited the integration of agentic AI into operating systems as an existential threat to user privacy. She argued that Windows, Android, and macOS platforms now allow AI agents to scan screen information, thereby undermining Signal's capacity to guarantee data privacy. Whittaker stated that sensitive data gets captured and stored by local databases or remote servers under OS-level AI control. This process removes the data from Signal's oversight and jeopardizes the privacy of all conversation participants.

Another item, from TechCrunch AI: Nvidia presented conflicting narratives this week as strong financial results clashed with external pressure to reinstate former CEO Jensen Huang. The company reported soaring revenue and beat investor expectations while interim CEO Colette Kress assured the market that the current leadership team had control. During the same period, Elon Musk and other technology executives publicly campaigned for Huang’s return, circulating a petition and highlighting the former CEO’s own statements that he was prepared to resume his role. This dual dynamic created what analysts described as a tale of two Nvidias, with one narrative focused on operational stability and the other on executive upheaval.

Also in the mix, from The Verge: Amazon is placing labels on shipments to warn New York City customers that the Delivery Protection Act could add up to five dollars to their orders. The proposed legislation would ban the third-party delivery arrangements Amazon currently uses and require last-mile firms to license their warehouses and directly employ workers. Earlier this week, the company began displaying similar warnings in its mobile app and on its website, and it has now extended those messages to shipping labels. Amazon argues that the bill would result in slower and more expensive delivery for millions of residents and has threatened to move its warehouses outside the city.

Shifting gears, from The Verge: Diplomats at the United Nations in Geneva agreed to begin discussing potential regulations for autonomous weapons after a week of negotiations that yielded limited progress. The compromise grants member states two years to propose rules, allowing some regulations to remain optional rather than mandatory. This timeline extends beyond the one-year deadline for legally binding agreements urged by the International Committee of the Red Cross, who emphasized the urgent need for restrictions due to civilian casualties in conflicts across Ukraine, Gaza, and Sudan. Russia, Israel, and the United States acted as primary obstacles to a faster resolution, with Russia delaying proceedings by challenging the agenda and the United States initially proposing a five-year window for nonbinding guidelines.
