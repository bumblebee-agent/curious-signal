## The News

The main reports are dated October 31, 2026.

Creator-built AI workflows and discovery tools lead the curated feed, with practical demonstrations outpacing independent validation.

First, from Hacker News: The author of the Karpathy blog says frustration with YouTube’s recommendations led to HackerTube, an alternative video-discovery site built around YouTube videos recommended on Hacker News. In an October 31, 2026 post, the author says Opus 5.5 built and deployed the site in roughly two hours. HackerTube contains 6,454 videos across 150 topics, drawn from about 40,000 comments recommending videos between 2007 and 2026. The author says the workflow used Sonnet to help filter out spam and low-quality content. HackerTube replaces uploaded thumbnails with frames from each video’s midpoint and offers personalized recommendations without accounts, keeping state locally in the browser session.

Across 18 sampled comments: Reaction is largely enthusiastic: commenters find HN-based video curation more relevant and less clickbait-heavy than their YouTube feeds.

Next, from r/comfyui: A creator shares a Halloween music video made with Claude operating their VRGDG Video Builder in ComfyUI using MiniMax H3. They report 19 scenes totaling 2:29, 80 renders and roughly 12 GPU hours. Claude handled lyrics, shot planning, rendering and take review; the creator supplied the concept, prepared music and locations, selected characters, requested revisions and applied a final Topaz AI enhancement. The post describes a completed workflow, not an unresolved support request, and distinguishes its single-scene workflow from the builder needed for a full video.

Across 5 sampled comments: The small discussion is mostly positive about the video builder, visual quality, and expressive performances. Criticism focuses on exaggerated singing expressions and fatigue with similar-sounding AI music videos.

Also, from Hacker News: Bootgpt runs a tiny neural network directly from a PC’s boot sector, generating fictional Linux boot logs rather than loading an operating system. The handwritten assembly runtime fits into 510 bytes and loads an 11.5 KB int8 model from the following 23 disk sectors. The network runs in 16-bit real mode on a 386 without an operating system or C runtime. The character-level model uses eight characters of context and one hidden layer to produce plausible-looking timestamps, PCI entries and ACPI events for nonexistent hardware. A training script generates 3 MB of synthetic boot logs and quantizes the model using power-of-two scales. The project reports that Python’s simulation of the integer inference and sampling matches the assembly output character for character.

Then, from Hacker News: Linux kernel vulnerability CVE-2026-98432 allows an unauthenticated remote attacker to reach a use-after-free flaw during an IPv4 TCP handshake, before application authentication. The Linux kernel security authority and Red Hat rate the vulnerability Critical, with a CVSS score of 9.8, because potential consequences include memory disclosure, crashes and remote code execution. Anthropic says its Claude model developed an unpublished proof-of-concept exploit that executes commands as root on servers with a multipath TCP listener. The Go Security Team says Linux programs using Go 1.24 or later defaults can open these listeners where the kernel permits MPTCP.

Across 18 sampled comments: The thread mixes alarm over reported Linux kernel exploitability with frustration about Ubuntu and RHEL patch availability.

Worth noting, from r/LocalLLaMA: The creator presents QwenPU, claiming to run Karpathy’s stories260k model on an NPU compiled into Qwen 3.5-4B’s weights. The post says the NPU performs bit-exact matrix multiplication, softmax and attention, with the inner model’s weights stored in Qwen’s memory and no external tools. A full writeup is pending, with optimizations and size reductions still underway; no performance measurements are supplied.

Another item, from Hacker News: Koppar has designed a four-layer LTE modem board intended to give a computer mobile data, SMS and voice calls through one USB-C connection. The design combines a Quectel EC25-EUX modem with an RP2040 microcontroller intended to expose call audio as a USB microphone and speaker, allowing calls to reach a headset, script or AI agent. The Rev B design reports zero design-rule errors and zero unconnected connections in KiCad 10, but hardware validation remains pending. The boards are being ordered, and bring-up, sound-card firmware development, and the first call and text remain future steps. The revision corrects antenna routing, power-converter placement and SIM-socket orientation problems found in the initial design.

Across 18 sampled comments: Commenters like the prospect of physical-SIM calls and texts on a computer, including scripted or AI-agent call handling.

Also in the mix, from TechCrunch AI: Apple CEO John Ternus defended the company’s AI strategy in an interview with Wired’s Steven Levy, saying he likes Apple’s position despite criticism that the company has lagged behind competitors. Apple’s more powerful Siri launched this summer after delays, and Ternus became CEO in September. Ternus said Apple will incorporate AI into new products but will prioritize useful experiences over AI for its own sake. Levy asked whether AI agents could make apps unnecessary, and Ternus acknowledged the potential convenience while emphasizing that product creation remains a deeply human and personal pursuit.

Cecelia Condit’s 1983 film Possibly in Michigan combines cannibalism and feminist musical themes in 12 minutes, finding a wider audience decades later through YouTube and Reddit. Homemade Gatorade mixes stiff animation and heavily processed video in a road-trip short that straddles horror and comedy. Another update, from The Verge: The Verge staff’s everyday keyboards range from basic Apple models to extensively customized mechanical builds, with comfort, noise, desk space, and appearance driving their choices. Keychron and NuPhy recur among staff picks, while split Kinesis keyboards serve writers with wrist pain or repetitive stress injuries. Senior reporter Jay Peters favors the Kinesis Freestyle Pro with tenting accessories and palm rests after trying numerous ergonomic keyboards. Senior internet typist Elizabeth Lopatto uses the split Kinesis Gaming Freestyle Edge RGB with deliberately loud Cherry MX Blue switches. Editor at large David Pierce takes the opposite approach to noise, avoiding mechanical keyboards during calls and switching from Logitech’s MX Keys S to an Apple Magic Keyboard for Touch ID.

Looking ahead, from r/StableDiffusion: A comparison of Qwen Image 2.1, FLUX and FIBO 2 found that Qwen delivered the strongest overall results across local image-editing tasks, while FLUX ran about twice as fast as Qwen in the presenter’s tests. The presenter ran the edits locally in ComfyUI without cloud processing or subscriptions. Qwen and FIBO 2 performed well on photo restoration, cleaning up a damaged photograph from around 1900 while preserving faces. FLUX left substantial scratches in the restoration test. The presenter recommended restoring photographs before colorizing them and cautioned that AI enlargement redraws plausible details rather than recovering authentic missing information.

Across 7 sampled comments: Several commenters report graininess or patterned artifacts in Qwen2.1 image edits. Reaction is mixed: users appreciate free, zero-login workflows, but the cause of the image-quality issues remains unclear.

Finally, from r/artificial: KoboldCpp 1.124 expands built-in music, speech and image generation while adding AI Horde chat-completions support and runtime fixes. The release integrates selected audio.cpp models, including YuE2 music generation with optional language models for writing lyrics. New speech models include BreezeTTS 2, Qwen3-TTS Base and VoiceDesign, VoxCPM2, CosyVoice 3, Fish Audio S2 Pro and others, with voice cloning and instruction support varying by model. MusicUI supports the new models and adds a Whisper-powered transcription tab for reference transcripts. Image-generation support expands to MOSS Image, Fibo 2 Edit and Flux 3 Klein.
