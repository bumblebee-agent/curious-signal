### A training engine for your local model, using your idle compute

Songe is a local learning layer that uses corrections, preferences, retries and tool errors from AI-agent conversations to personalize a model. Songe sits between an OpenAI-compatible agent and a local model server, records interactions and learns during idle periods. The project says all processing stays on the machine, with no account, telemetry or cloud service. Songe offers two learning modes. A Quick Dream adds learned preferences to model instructions in minutes, while a Deep Dream trains a small adapter using synthetic practice scenes rather than real conversations. Songe evaluates the trained version against the current version and adopts it only if checks show clear improvement without degrading successful behavior. Users can remove preferences or roll back learned versions. Songe lists support for LM Studio, vLLM, Ollama, llama.cpp and mlx-lm, with automatic agent setup for opencode and pi. End-to-end testing covers LM Studio on macOS, vLLM on Linux, both named agents, and training on Apple Silicon and NVIDIA hardware. AMD training is listed as supported but not end-to-end tested, while machines without GPUs can serve models and record interactions but cannot train. Songe requires Python 3.11 or later and installs a background service that starts at login. Learning runs only when the agent is quiet and memory and battery conditions permit, and learning stops when the user returns. Songe preserves original models, backs up modified agent settings and provides controls to pause learning, delete recorded conversations and uninstall the service. The project uses the Apache 2.0 license. ([A training engine for your local model, using your idle compute](https://github.com/sous-labs/songe)).

### Even the best AI agents struggle with everyday life

ShellBench, a new benchmark for everyday AI agent work, found that even the strongest models tested from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta failed more than a third of attempts. Across all tested models, 15.7% of attempts violated at least one safety requirement, while roughly 10% of otherwise correct attempts also failed a safety criterion. Researchers developed ShellBench using more than 10 million anonymized, real-world agent interactions to evaluate multi-step personal tasks involving messy emails, calendars, spreadsheets, receipts, and other records. OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra achieved the highest score, but the model completed all three repetitions of a task successfully less than half the time. Agents failed to adequately account for school pickup in 79.5% of relevant attempts and recommended duplicate or incorrect invoice payments in 40.3% of attempts on another task. Model choice had the greatest overall effect on performance, but the software controlling tool use and actions also substantially affected results. Meta’s Muse Wonder Full scored 56% in the Pi harness and 38.1% in Claude Code. The researchers did not test models inside the native Muse, Dots, or Claude Duet products, so the findings do not directly establish those consumer assistants’ reliability. ([Even the best AI agents struggle with everyday life](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/even-the-best-ai-agents-struggle-with-everyday-life/)).

### Huawei found guilty of stealing trade secrets

A federal jury in New York found Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 criminal counts in a case involving the theft of US trade secrets, according to NBC News and Bloomberg. The years-long case accused Huawei of stealing source code and proprietary technology from companies including T-Mobile, Cisco and Motorola. The convictions included money laundering, bank fraud, wire fraud and conspiracy to defraud the US. The jury acquitted Huawei of criminal racketeering conspiracy and obstruction of justice. Huawei plans to appeal and could face fines, forfeiture and restitution. A Huawei spokesperson told NBC News that the charges were “without legal or factual merit.” ([Huawei found guilty of stealing trade secrets](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1041820/huawei-guilty-trade-secrets-trial)).

### Show HN: Mason – A lightweight Postgres read-replica, built on columnar storage

Mason is an Apache-2.0 open-source tool positioned as a lightweight replacement for a Postgres read replica for analytical workloads. Mason runs as a single Node.js process, stores configuration and synchronized data locally, and uses DuckDB’s columnar engine while rewriting queries to preserve Postgres syntax and behavior. Mason’s published benchmarks report substantial gains for queries that aggregate many rows across a few columns. A grouping query over 10 million rows took 0.193 seconds in Mason versus 15.7 seconds on the Postgres primary, an 81-fold speedup. Other published tests showed analytical speedups of 16 to 467 times, but primary-key lookups were 2.8 to six times slower. The tests used cold starts and reported median results across three runs, and actual performance depends on data size and distribution. Users can select tables and columns to synchronize through on-demand updates, polling or logical replication. Mason includes per-user role-based access controls, row-level security, query logging and a built-in MCP server for AI agents. Mason also offers automatically updated generated tables, monitoring integrations, IP allowlists, TLS and a web interface for managing tables and users. ([Show HN: Mason – A lightweight Postgres read-replica, built on columnar storage](https://masondata.ai/)).

## Tools and Smaller Signals

- [Microduck Obstacle Avoidance: A Sim2Real Workflow with Isaac Lab and Newton](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/microduck-obstacle-avoidance): Microduck can learn local obstacle avoidance without retraining its walking policy through a workflow using NVIDIA Isaac Lab and Newton simulation. A separate navigation controller adjusts gamepad-requested forward, lateral and turning velocities at 50 Hz, while the frozen walking policy handles balance and joint actions. The controller uses an 8×8 time-of-flight sensor that measures depth at 15 Hz. Simulated training adds noise, delays and missing readings, while randomized courses combine cones with SimReady boxes, bins and cabinets. Rewards favor safe progress toward goals and penalize collisions, falls, abrupt commands and risky approaches. The training example uses reinforcement learning across 16,384 parallel environments for 1,500 iterations, although smaller configurations are supported. A reported NVIDIA DGX Spark run took approximately six hours with 4,096 environments and 3,000 updates. The deployment tools run the exported navigation model on a laptop and send adjusted commands to the robot’s walking policy. A stationary dry run streams sensor data without commanding movement before supervised testing. Deployment safeguards include a default forward-speed cap of 0.15 metres per second, close-obstacle braking and stopping when inputs become stale.
- [How iFood scales coding agents across 15,000 repositories](https://openai.com/business/customer-stories/ifood): iFood says its Remote Agents Platform has completed more than 25,000 engineering tasks across an ecosystem of more than 15,000 code repositories, consuming over 27 billion tokens at an average cost of US$0.04 per run. The company reports that its internal security platform more than doubled both fix accuracy and the number of vulnerabilities resolved after adopting the system. The platform lets teams submit prompts and environment requirements through a REST API, delegates execution to isolated remote sandboxes, and returns results asynchronously, including merge requests and generated artifacts. Deterministic steps handle repository cloning, branch setup and publication, while GPT‑5.6 Luna performs the agentic coding work. OpenAI’s Structured Outputs formats session results according to a predefined contract to support automated assessment of success or failure. Victor Coelho, senior software engineer at iFood, says the shared runtime replaces fragmented team-level solutions and allows developers to delegate routine work without continuously monitoring execution. iFood plans to expand the platform to more internal use cases, with per-task cost guiding the sustainability of that expansion.
- [Agentic system personalizes physical controls to help users “DJ their art”](https://news.mit.edu/2026/tangimap-agentic-system-personalizes-physical-controls-help-users-dj-their-art-1124): TangiMap uses AI to repurpose physical knobs, faders and buttons for software tasks, bringing tactile controls to photo editing, music performance, 3D modeling and animation. MIT doctoral student and CSAIL researcher Yunyi Zhu developed the system with Boston University assistant professor Chang Xiao. TangiMap gathers context about an application, a user’s habits and an input device through software interfaces or screenshot analysis, then uses a large language model to map digital functions to physical controls. The researchers demonstrated mixers controlling color adjustments in Adobe Lightroom and model properties in Blender, alongside a control pad triggering expressions and movements in Adobe Character Animator. Physical controls could support eyes-free operation and simultaneous adjustments, but improvements to user experience remain unverified. The researchers created custom interfaces for each application and plan to pursue user testing, more ergonomic mappings and a simpler setup process that could generate a device interface from a photograph.
- [Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B](https://prismml.com/news/bonsai-2-5-27b): PrismML has introduced Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, a Qwen3.8 27B-based model that retains its predecessor’s 5.9 GB footprint while targeting stronger local coding agents and multi-step tool use. PrismML says the update reduces excessive thinking and repetitive loops, completing tasks in fewer turns and with fewer tokens. PrismML’s reported evaluations show SWE-bench Verified scores rising from 60.8 to 75.6 and AppWorld scores increasing from 51.2 to 90.5 compared with Bonsai 2 27B. The newer model narrows the gap with the full-precision base model on several benchmarks, although its LiveCodeBench v6 score slips from 83.8 to 83.0. PrismML reports generation speeds of 291 tokens per second on an NVIDIA H100 and 204 tokens per second on an RTX 4090 with multi-token prediction. Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B supports NVIDIA and Intel GPUs through llama.cpp and Apple devices through MLX. The model weights are available under the Apache 2.0 license, with local deployment aimed at applications that handle private files and tools on-device.
